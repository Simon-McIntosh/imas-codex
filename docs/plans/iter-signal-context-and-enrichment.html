Plan · ITER plant data

ITER plant signals carry context and are enriched by group

The archive lists about a million plant variables and describes almost none of them. This plan gives each signal context from ITER's own records, enriches signals a component and a field pattern at a time, and ends with an agent finding a plant signal by description and reading it.

§1 — Where this starts

The scanner, loader, plotting and name parser landed under the plan codac-uda-signals. Its full-archive census counted 988,145 variables on the slow archive and 6,861 on the fast one, with none in common. The archive's type metadata gives the same description, "Signal value", for every variable measured, and refuses metadata altogether for about one variable in eight. A second archive interface returns a label for 70 of a 200-variable sample and a unit for some of them. The first 200 signals catalogued are five controllers that each expose the same forty fields.

A test question to the ITER document assistant about three of those names matched one component conditionally, one probably and one not at all, and pointed to the documents that hold the meaning: a per-system signal list, a per-system data file from which control-system variables are generated, a control functional specification, and the plant breakdown structure. A build worker cannot reach any of them: the document system redirects an unauthenticated request to a login page.

The lead has decided four things. ICP and IDM content may be curated into the graph as metadata and links. Every node and link so created references the ITER facility and is covered by the facility-scoped protected backup, and none of it enters the repository. Work that reads that content runs on the locally served model inside ITER, once a benchmark shows that lane is capable. Signals are enriched by component and by field pattern, not one at a time.

The existing signals discovery pipeline already enriches a source once and fills a template for each member, and already exports and pushes graph archives per facility. This plan uses both.

§2 — Every configured model seat names its model's latest version

Delivers. Each model seat the project configuration declares names the latest available version of the model it intends, or the landing record states why a seat stays on an earlier one. Seats that route to the locally served model name the model that lane serves today.

Done when. The landing record lists every declared seat with the model it named before, the latest version found and where that was established, and the model it names after; the configuration tests pass with no added failure against the base revision; and each changed seat answers one live request. Negative control: a seat naming a model its route does not serve fails its live request and is reported, not silently left.

§3 — The local model and a paid model are compared on signal enrichment

Delivers. A comparison of the relative benefit of the locally served DeepSeek model and a paid model on the signals discovery pipeline's enrichment work, so the lead can decide which carries the enrichment of ITER signals once their context exists. One sample is used: signals of another facility that the pipeline has already enriched. Each is enriched again through the pipeline's own enrichment stage with its normal full context, once by each model, with the same prompts and the same retrieved context. No signal is enriched from its name alone; that is not an achievable task and is not measured.

Done when. For at least two hundred already-enriched signals of another facility, chosen before the run across several physics domains, the landing record gives for each model: the share of responses that parse as the pipeline's structured output without repair; the share whose physics domain and unit agree with the stored values; the cost per signal and in total, with token counts; the calls per minute and wall time; and the count of failed or empty responses. Between the two it gives a blind judged comparison of the pair of descriptions for each signal, as local better, paid better or equivalent, with the judge not told which is which, forty pairs also read by hand and the hand reading's agreement with the judge stated; and the number of signals where either description is wrong against the signal's context. The paid model is named, and its spend is estimated from ten signals before the run and stays under twenty US dollars. The test signals' stored enrichment is left unchanged; the trial writes to a private store. The run executed on a compute partition (rigel). The lead's choice of model for ITER enrichment is recorded in this plan before section 6 is dispatched. Negative control: a response that fails to parse is counted as a failure, never repaired by hand and counted as a success; the judge's verdicts are unchanged when the two descriptions are presented in the opposite order for forty pairs, or the disagreement rate is reported; and the stored enrichment of the other facility's signals is byte-identical before and after.

§4 — One plant system's signals carry context from ICP and IDM

Delivers. A link is marked as exact when the source names the archive variable, as derived when it was joined through a component number, and as absent when no source row was found. The content reaches the graph from ICP and IDM through the read-only client that the plan icp-api-access delivers, which can issue no write, and it does not enter the repository. The signal list and the functional specification are read from the private store that plan's section 5 filled on 2026-11-18 by fetching the seven named pilot documents: the signal list as a spreadsheet of three sheets and the other six as PDF files. No separate data file is among them, so none is imported. Plant structure records come through its section 3. The function-category codes and their official meanings are imported with it, so a component's category has a name.

Done when. The landing record gives the number of source rows imported per source, the number of the pilot system's signals with an exact link, a derived link and no link, and twenty links of each present kind checked by hand against the source; every imported node and link reaches the ITER facility node; a second import adds none; and a search of the tracked tree for the imported identifiers and a sample of imported titles finds none. Graph-marked statements pass. Negative control: a signal whose component number appears in no source row receives no link, and a row that names a different system links to no pilot signal. On 2026-11-18 the lead decided this section lands without an archive of its own: the graph's weekly archive carries the imported content, and the import can be repeated from the documents on disk.

§5 — Catalogued signals are grouped by component and by field pattern

Delivers. Every catalogued archive variable, whether in the graph or only in the private catalogue store, is either in a component group or in an explicit ungrouped state, and components that expose the same set of fields share a field pattern. The grouping is derived from the parsed variable names and the archive metadata. For the signals that are in the graph it is stored there under the ITER facility, and it is recomputed without duplicates when the catalogue grows. It is run first on the partial catalogue, while the archive scan is still filling the private store, and run again when the scan completes.

Done when. For each run the landing record states the fraction of the archive the private store held, and gives the number of variables, the number grouped and the number ungrouped, the number of component groups and field patterns; the distribution of group size; the share of signals whose group has a single member; and the twenty largest patterns with their field counts, using invented names in tracked files. The number of group descriptions and pattern descriptions that enrichment would need is stated beside the number of signals. A second run adds no groups. Negative control: a signal whose name does not parse is placed in an explicit ungrouped state and is counted, never attached to another component's group.

§6 — The pilot system's signals are enriched by group and the result is measured

Delivers. Each component of the pilot system that has linked context has one description written from it, each field pattern whose members have linked context has one description of what its fields mean, and every member signal with linked context carries a description composed from the two. A description states the signal's quantity, unit and role where the vocabulary of section 11 decided them, and leaves out any of the three that is undecided or in conflict. A component or pattern with no linked context has no description and is counted as awaiting context. The language model that writes them is the locally served model with the grounded settings on the primary branch, which the lead confirmed for this pilot on 2026-11-17. A signal with no linked context is not enriched: it is marked as awaiting context and counted. Component and pattern descriptions reach members through the one group enrichment mechanism the discovery pipeline already has, keeping its claim, cost and status behaviour.

Done when. The landing record gives the number of component descriptions, pattern descriptions and member descriptions written and the number of language-model calls and their cost; for forty member signals chosen before the run, a hand comparison against the functional specification with the count that are right, partly right and wrong, where at most two of the forty are wrong and at least thirty-two are right. A description is wrong when it states something the functional specification contradicts, or something that neither the specification nor the signal's own recorded sources support; a value the vocabulary decided counts as supported by the source credited for it, partly right when everything it states is supported but it omits a quantity, unit or role that the vocabulary decided for that signal, and right otherwise; leaving out a value the vocabulary did not decide does not lower the grade. The bar is read by hand, the same instrument on which the refinement round found none of the paid model's forty descriptions wrong and 26 of the local model's; the lead set the bar at two wrong and thirty-two right on 2026-11-17. A section that misses the bar is delivered as failed on quality, with the forty readings, for the lead to decide on; the number of signals, of components and of field patterns left awaiting context, each with its described count summing to the pilot's total of that kind, where the pilot's field patterns are those with at least one pilot member; the number of member descriptions that state each of the three vocabulary values, none of them stating a value the vocabulary does not hold for that signal; and the projected call count and cost for the whole catalogue from the grouping in section 5. A search recorded in the landing record finds one path by which a group description reaches its members. The projection is in this plan and accepted by the lead before section 7 is dispatched. Negative control: a member of a group whose component description failed is reported in a failed state and carries no composed description.

Signals of systems with no imported context are not enriched; they are marked as awaiting context, counted by plant system, and enriched when their context arrives. The landing record states how much of the catalogue that is, so the next systems to import context for can be chosen.

A search for a quantity of another facility still returns that facility's signal. The run executed on rigel. Negative control: a signal whose enrichment failed is reported in a failed state and is not counted as enriched.

§8 — An agent finds an ITER plant signal and reads it through the server

Delivers. Through the project's server, a search for a described quantity returns an ITER plant signal with its component and context links, and a facility read loads it by identity over a bounded window. The read returns metadata, basic statistics and a reference to an internal file holding the arrays, with arrays inlined only under a strict byte limit. It accepts no host or endpoint from the caller, and is absent in the data-dictionary-only mode. The search is the existing signal search and adds no tool. The read is the one tool added, because no existing tool returns archive samples.

Done when. Through a running server, a search for a described quantity returns a pilot-system signal and the read returns its statistics for a two-second window, both in the landing record; the tool list in data-dictionary-only mode does not contain the read; a response never exceeds the transport's bounded size; and the landing record gives the server's tool count before and after, which differ by one, and names any existing tool the read could have been merged into with the reason it was not. Negative control: a call supplying an endpoint, and a call with an unbounded window, are each refused.

Done when. Each pilot signal carries a measured quantity, a unit and a role from a closed vocabulary

Delivers. Before any description is written, each signal of the pilot system carries three values from a small closed vocabulary held in the schema: the measured quantity (such as current, temperature, flow or valve position), its unit, and its role (measurement, setpoint, state, alarm or command), each with the source that decided it. There are four sources, in this order of precedence: a linked signal-list row, the controls metadata the signal already carries, the unit the archive gives (for the unit only), and the field pattern of its name. For each value a signal has exactly one outcome: decided, when every source that gives the value gives the same one, credited to the first such source in that order; conflict, when two sources give different values, in which case none is stored; or undecided, when no source gives one. Nothing is guessed. The values are stored on the signal apart from its description, so search can match on them exactly and a description can be checked against them. The session coordinating standard names measured that missing context and model-written guesses caused most of its wrong text, and advised assigning a closed vocabulary before prose; the lead chose this on 2026-11-19. Standard names are not assigned here.

Done when. The landing record gives the vocabulary's members; for the 1,766 pilot signals, for each of the three values, the number decided, split by the source credited, the number in conflict and the number undecided, these figures summing to 1,766; and for sixty signals chosen before the run and written to a private file whose digest is in the landing record, fifteen per credited source, or every such signal where a source is credited for fewer, a hand reading against that source with the count of values that agree, where at most one value in twenty read disagrees. For every signal whose unit is decided, the stored unit equals the unit each of its sources gives, with the number of signals compared. A second assignment changes no signal. Through the server's signal search, a query for one quantity and one role on the pilot system returns only signals carrying both. No real signal name appears in a tracked file; rules are tested against invented names. Graph-marked statements pass. Negative control: an invented signal whose field pattern is in no rule and which has no metadata and no linked row is recorded as undecided for all three values; and a signal whose sources disagree on the unit is recorded as a conflict and counted, not resolved silently.

Each field code has a stated meaning from a source

Delivers. A table of the field codes that end archive variable names, each with what it means: the role a signal with that code plays (measurement, setpoint, state, alarm or command, from the closed vocabulary of section 11), a one-line plain meaning, and where the code's meaning depends on the kind of component, the meaning for each kind. Each entry records the source that established it: a signal-list row or its legend, the controls metadata of signals carrying the code, or the functional specification. A code no source establishes has no entry and is counted; nothing is inferred from the letters of the code. The table is held in the schema's data, apart from any description; section 13 applies it across the plant. The landed work shows why it is needed: in the landed pilot descriptions, sibling signals on one component with different field codes were seen to carry the same text; the vocabulary decided a role for 202 signals, which the sibling-relation run of 2026-11-21 split as alarm 42, command 80, measurement 3, state 77 and setpoint 0; and with no setpoint no signal can be related to the one it is measured against.

Done when. The landing record gives the number of distinct field codes in the catalogue; the number with an entry, split by the source that established it, and the number without, summing to the number of distinct codes; the number of catalogued signals whose code has an entry; and the number of entries giving each role, with the codes examined for the setpoint role and, where none establishes it, the reason in plain words. Thirty table entries chosen before the run and written to a private file whose digest is in the landing record, ten per establishing source or all where a source has fewer, are read by hand against that source, one written verdict and reason each, with at most one wrong. Building the table a second time changes no entry. No real signal name or code meaning taken from a private document appears in a tracked file; the table's private rows live in private configuration and tests use invented codes. Graph-marked statements pass. Negative control: an invented code with no source has no entry and its signals stay undecided; a code whose meaning differs by component kind gives each kind its own role and a signal of a third kind none; two sources that give one code different roles for the same component kind produce a conflict and no entry; and a reading file in which every verdict is produced by a script is not a reading.

Roles are decided across the plant from the field-code table

Delivers. The vocabulary assignment of section 11 run over every catalogued signal of the facility that is in the graph, as a job on rigel, with the field-code table of section 12 as its field-pattern source, where it has so far been run on the pilot system alone. Values are decided only where a source gives them; nothing is guessed. Whether any signal is decided as a setpoint is a property of the sources, which this section reports and does not force.

Done when. The landing record gives, for each of quantity, unit and role, the number of signals decided split by the source credited, in conflict, and undecided, summing to the number of catalogued signals of the facility in the graph, which the record states; and the number of signals decided as each role, including setpoint. For the pilot system the role and quantity decided before this section are compared with those after it: the number unchanged, newly decided, and changed, with every changed value listed privately and read by hand against the signal's own sources, one written verdict and reason each, and none wrong: a value the earlier assignment decided correctly and this section changed wrongly fails the section. Thirty signals whose role this section newly decided, chosen before the run and written to a private file whose digest is in the landing record, ten per credited source or all where a source has fewer, are read by hand against their own metadata, one written verdict and reason each, with at most two wrong. A second assignment changes no signal. Where at least one signal is decided as a setpoint, a server search for the setpoint role on a system that holds one returns it and only signals carrying the role; where none is, the record says so and names the section 12 entries that explain why. Graph-marked statements pass. No real signal name appears in a tracked file. Negative control: a signal whose field code has no table entry and no other source stays undecided; a pilot value changed by hand in a scratch run to contradict its source is caught by the pilot comparison; and a reading file in which every verdict is produced by a script is not a reading.

§ Decisions

Which model writes ITER signal descriptions in the discovery pipeline?

The comparison in section 3 on 200 signals with identical full-context prompts: the local model delivered 130 of 200 and none of its 20 raw responses parsed without repair; the paid model delivered 200 of 200 with all 20 parsing, for 0.95 US dollars.

This records the choice made on 2026-11-15 and what was then measured; it no longer governs sections 6 and 7, which are held by the open decision local-model-confirmed-on-quality. The lead reverted an earlier choice of the paid model so that ICP-derived context stays inside ITER, and asked for the pipeline to be refined for the locally served model. With the pipeline's defaults that model parsed 0 of 20 responses unrepaired and delivered 130 of 200 descriptions, against 20 of 20 and 200 of 200 for the paid model. After refinement (one signal per call, a prompt fitted to the model, one re-ask on a validation failure) 197 of 200 raw responses parse and all 200 are delivered; schema-constrained output lowered parsing and is left off. Quality on the same 200 signals: in a blind reading of 40 pairs sealed before the judge ran, the paid model's description was preferred in 27 and the local model's in none, with 13 ties, and 26 of the local model's 40 descriptions were read as wrong against none of the paid model's; detail not supported by the signal's context was the dominant defect. The automated judge put 111 of the local model's 200 and 32 of the paid model's as wrong, read preference as nearly even (paid 75, local 71, tie 54), and agreed with the hand reading on 17 of 40 pairs.

Is a table of field-code meanings, applied plant-wide to decide roles, built before the full-catalogue enrichment and the sibling-signal relations?

Pilot enrichment gave sibling signals identical descriptions, the vocabulary decided a role for 202 signals and no setpoint, and the sibling-relation section wrote no relation for want of roles.

The lead chose on 2026-11-21 to plan and build field-code meanings next.

Is the projected call count and cost of enriching the whole catalogue accepted?

Decided after section 6 reports the pilot's measured cost and the projection from the grouping.

Is the local lane accepted to build the sections that read ICP and IDM content?

Decided after section 3 reports its comparison. Until then no ICP or IDM content is given to any worker.

Narrowed by the lead's later rulings on 2026-11-15. Build workers that develop and test the pipeline, including the ICP client, may run on any lane; the lead's own account is the identity for ICP access, and that access is read-only by code. Which model writes ITER signal descriptions from ICP-derived context is not settled here: it is the open decision local-model-confirmed-on-quality. ICP-derived context stays inside ITER unless the lead chooses the paid model there, and no section that would send that context to a model is built until that decision is answered.

After the refinement result, which model writes ITER signal descriptions?

Reliability and quality were measured in three rounds on the same 200 signals of another facility with identical context. With the pipeline's defaults the locally served model parsed 0 of 20 raw responses and delivered 130 of 200. After a reliability round (one signal per call, a fitted prompt, one re-ask) 197 of 200 parsed and all 200 were delivered, but a sealed blind hand reading found 26 of 40 descriptions wrong against none of the paid model's. After a correctness round (grounding in the supplied context, a one-sentence limit, a second local pass with reasoning that removes unsupported detail) the hand reading found 2 wrong, 1 partly right and 37 right on the original forty, and 1, 2 and 37 on a fresh forty chosen before any run; the paid model had none wrong on either, and blind preference was paid 23, local 4, tie 13 on the original forty and paid 16, local 15, tie 9 on the fresh one. On the full 200 the correctness configuration delivered 196 and 194 raw responses parsed, so four came back empty, and it is slower because it makes a second call per signal. The automated judge agreed with hand reading on 17 of 40 pairs, so its figures are not relied on. Evidence: docs/evidence/fragments/iter-signal-context-and-enrichment/refine-local-model-signal-enrichment.html and ground-local-signal-descriptions-in-context.html. On 2026-11-17 the lead chose to keep the local model and run one more round, aimed at the empty responses and at speed, before the correctness options are switched on; the lead also set section 6's quality bar at two wrong and thirty-two right of forty. The middle option below is that round. Choosing the paid model would send ICP-derived context outside ITER. Sections 6 and 7 are held on this decision until that round reports and the options are switched on.

The lead confirmed the local model for the ITER pilot on 2026-11-17, after the round they asked for delivered all 200 descriptions and a blind hand reading found 1 and 0 wrong of forty with 32 and 33 right, meeting their bar of at most two wrong and at least thirty-two right. ITER descriptions are written by the locally served model with the grounded settings on the primary branch, and ICP-derived context stays inside ITER.

Is a closed vocabulary of measured quantity, unit and role assigned to each signal before any description is written?

The session coordinating standard names measured that missing context and model-written guesses caused most of its wrong text, and advised a closed vocabulary before prose, so search can match on the quantity and a description can be checked against it.

The lead chose vocabulary first on 2026-11-19: quantity, unit and role are assigned by rule where a source decides them, and pilot enrichment waits on that.
