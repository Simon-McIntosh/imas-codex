§1 — Stop cycling means make the call, not grind it down

Two sessions on 2026-08-25 spent a day repairing residues row by row. Most of that work was correct and some of it was durable prevention, but the pattern it settled into — measure a class, repair it, find the producer, repair that — is a cycle that terminates only when someone decides it should. This plan exists to make that decision explicitly, on a number, once the number can be trusted.

It cannot be made yet, and the reason is not cost. 1,091 identities declare no origin, 278 of them live and accepted, so a regeneration scoped by origin cannot distinguish protected human curation from regenerable pipeline output. That is the prerequisite, and until it lands this plan has nothing to weigh.

§2 — What regeneration would and would not clear

The instinct behind the strategy is that a clean regenerate from sources beats an endless repair queue. That is right about mechanical residue and wrong about semantic residue, and the distinction decides how much regeneration is actually worth.

So regeneration is not a way to clear the semantic residue. The three remaining identity rows are questions about physics and vocabulary; a regenerate produces them again, from the same sources, in the same form. Framing the strategy as "get the residue small then regenerate to clear the rest" would therefore buy nothing for exactly the rows that are hardest.

What regeneration is worth is freshness: documentation and naming produced under current prompts, current grammar and current review criteria rather than accumulated across many rotations. That is a real benefit and it should be priced as such rather than as defect clearance.

§3 — The threshold this plan must state

A decision made on a feeling gets re-litigated. This plan produces a written threshold and applies it once. The quantities it needs, all of which become measurable after the origin backfill:

  • Regenerable population — identities with origin=pipeline after backfill, which is the true scope of any regenerate.
  • Protected population — origin=catalog_edit and the accepted documentation they carry, currently 2,096 and 2,031, which a regenerate must exclude rather than rebuild.
  • Cost per identity — measured, not estimated: compose is free on the local seat and review is the paid axis, so the figure is dominated by quorum draws.
  • Defects a regenerate would clear — stale documentation against current criteria, which is the only category it genuinely fixes.
  • Defects it would reintroduce — the honest counterweight, since every past rotation produced its own residue and there is no reason to assume the next one will not.

The last item deserves weight. This programme's own history is a sequence of generations each leaving defects the next had to repair. A regenerate that is not priced against the residue it will itself create is an optimistic forecast rather than a decision.

§ Decisions

If regeneration proceeds, what is its scope?

Should regeneration be expected to clear the remaining identity rows?

No, and this is recorded now so it is not assumed later. The dual-bound spelling choice, the field-map-grid locus and the reflector surface-versus-local-centre split are questions about physics and vocabulary, not artifacts of a particular generation. A regenerate re-derives each from the same source and poses the identical question. Regeneration is priced on freshness of documentation and naming under current criteria, never on clearing these three.

§ Followups

Measure the five quantities and put the scope choice to the lead with a recommendation

Blocked until origin is declared. Then measure the regenerable and protected populations, the measured cost per identity, what a regenerate clears, and what it would reintroduce — and present the scope decision with a recommendation and arguments rather than a menu.
