§1 — Why a chain of bounded decisions

The map stage writes the IMAS bindings, so it is the step whose errors reach a user's data. Today map_signals in imas_codex/ids/mapping.py makes one open-ended reasoning call per target section. That call returns a SignalMappingBatch whose entries carry a free-form Python transform_expression, a cocos_label, units and a confidence. The model therefore settles every part of a binding at once: which field, the sign, any 2π factor, the unit conversion and the index layout. Its prompt carries the source's representative COCOS, the DD's COCOS and the paths that need a flip, but nothing makes the sign it writes agree with them.

Most of those parts are not open questions. imas_codex/ids/transforms.py already computes a COCOS sign or factor deterministically, as cocos_sign(label, cocos_in=..., cocos_out=...), from a DD field's cocos_transformation_type and the two conventions. convert_units converts units through pint. What is missing is the source's own convention, which jt60sa-discovery-completion §56 now establishes per source and per quantity with evidence. Once that is known, the sign and factor of a COCOS-labelled field follow in code. What is left is genuinely uncertain, and each such slot is a bounded choice that Jev answers well: a sign of 1 or −1, a factor of 1, 2π or 1/2π, one of the unit readings pint offers, or one of a few index layouts. A local-lane model handles a slot only when it is not Jev-shaped, and anything still uncertain is escalated rather than guessed.

Candidate selection, which picks the target field, already works this way: Jev judges each candidate and the assign stage chooses among the shortlist. This plan extends the same discipline to the transform. Mapping stays opt-in in imas-codex discover FACILITY until this plan's proof lands, under the mapping-stage-default decision in facility-discovery-sequence.

§4 — Conventions settle what code can settle

A resolver runs before any model. It reads the source's convention from the graph, where signal-cocos-conventions persists it on the SignalSource: its cocos_class, its cocos_survivors, its cocos when the survivors are one convention, its confidence, and its HAS_GEOMETRY sensor geometry when present; a slot whose class is an angle class stays open while the source has no geometry. It reads the DD field's cocos_transformation_type, resolved through the field's ancestors because the DD carries a label on a structure such as magnetics/ip rather than on its data leaf, and the DD's own COCOS. A COCOS-labelled field gets its sign and factor from cocos_sign in code when every surviving source convention gives the same factor for that label: a psi_like field settles across survivors {3, 4}, while an ip_like field does not. A source whose class is none, bound to a field with no label or a one_like label, has no COCOS slot; its open slots are unit and index, and pint settles the unit from the source and target units. A per-member channel sign, such as a differential flux loop's, multiplies into the sign slot. An absent or split convention leaves its slot open: the resolver never defaults a value. Each settled slot records the convention's evidence and confidence beside its value, and the persisted conventions are injected into every later decision's state next to the DD COCOS label, so a Jev decision starts from what is known.

§5 — One Jev decision per open slot

Each slot still open becomes one bounded Jev question. The options are the slot's own: 1 or −1 for a sign; 1, 2π or 1/2π for a factor; the readings pint offers for an ambiguous unit; the candidate layouts for an index. The state is the source's detail, the target field's documentation and COCOS label, the injected conventions and the §6 context. It also carries what retrieval and judgment already measured for this pair: the candidate's retrieval score and Jev p_same_quantity from its MAPPING_CANDIDATE edge, the source's convention confidence, and its sensor geometry. Independent slots of one binding go in one request, as Jev runs them in parallel. A slot is accepted when its answer clears an acceptance threshold. The graph holds no written binding to calibrate against, so the threshold starts conservative and the §7 proof calibrates it against the recorded conventions. Below it, the slot goes to escalation with Jev's distribution as evidence. A slot that is not a bounded choice, such as an unusual index expression, goes to the local-lane model with the same state, and its answer must still name one of the composer's slot kinds. When the OpenRouter key is absent, the chain skips Jev and sends open slots to the local model, as the candidate stage already does.

§8 — Candidates match the source's COCOS class

The source's convention class is a strong filter on its targets. A source whose cocos_class is a DD label, at a confidence above the conventions stage's threshold, keeps only candidates whose resolved DD label is the same label: a confident psi_like flux loop is only ever bound to psi_like fields. A source whose class is none keeps only candidates with no label or a one_like label. A source whose class is compound, below threshold, or not yet judged is not filtered; its class enters the Jev pair judgment's state as a soft feature instead. Labels resolve through a field's ancestors, as in §4. The filter runs before the pair judgment in imas_codex/ids/candidates.py, so a filtered candidate costs no Jev call, and again where the assign stage chooses homes, so a candidate written before the source's convention was settled cannot be chosen against it. One-to-many is kept: the filter narrows each home's candidates and never chooses among homes. Each source records how many candidates the filter removed, so a filter that removes a true home shows in the §7 proof rather than silently.

§ Decisions

Do draft bindings of a source's siblings enter later decisions' state?

A draft binding is a MAPS_TO_IMAS edge under an IMASMapping at status generated. Injecting the drafts of the same sensor family, such as a probe's position already drafted to magnetics/b_field_pol_probe(i)/position, keeps a probe's measurement and geometry on the same struct-array index and the family's signs consistent.

Lead ruling 2026-10-09, accepting the recommendation: injecting a sensor family's draft bindings, labelled as drafts, keeps a probe's measurement and geometry on the same struct-array index and the family's signs consistent; a wrong draft that propagates is caught by validation and the §7 proof rather than by withholding sibling context.
