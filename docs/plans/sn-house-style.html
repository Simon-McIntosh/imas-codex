Plan · standard names · S18

One definition, one notation

The catalog defines the flux-surface average in five notations, one of them wrong, and opens its WEST entries in four styles. Each entry passed review on its own, so consistency has to be authored once and then enforced. Choosing the definitions is the coordinator's and the lead's work; applying them is the pipeline's.

§1 — Why a house style, and why it is authored

The measurements are in the consistency census. In short: 72 entries re-derive the flux-surface average, 57 the volume average and 35 the normalized toroidal-flux coordinate. The 80 boundary-locus names gloss "plasma boundary" eight different ways, and 24 of them equate it with the separatrix, which is wrong for a limited plasma. The poloidal field is written three ways. None of this is visible to per-name review, which is why WEST scores a median 0.94 on documentation while reading as inconsistent.

An LLM asked to "be consistent" with a handful of siblings will drift again on the next batch. What converges is a written authority: one entry per shared concept, stating its definition, its one equation, its symbols and the phrases it must never take. Choosing those entries is physics judgement. It is done once, in review with the lead, and is not a pipeline run. Everything downstream — generation, review, the lint and the harmonize command — reads that authority and never restates it.

§2 — The conventions registry, carried by the grammar

The lead ruled on 2026-10-12 that the registry lives in ISN now, beside the vocabularies it is keyed by, and is released before the WEST re-cut. Its files, model, loader and selecting accessor are built under imas-standard-names:concept-and-notation-registry §2, at imas_standard_names/grammar/vocabularies/conventions/. imas-codex holds no copy. It reads the registry through the ISN accessor, which it can do as soon as the registry lands, because pyproject.toml already sources ISN as an editable path. There are three files, each keyed by the grammar tokens a name's parse produces, so the entries that apply to a name are selected from its parse and never from a text search.

§3 — Authoring the entries: the coordinator polish a pipeline cannot do

The coordinator drafts every entry needed by the WEST roster first, then the wider corpus, and lands them in ISN under concept-and-notation-registry §3. Each draft starts from the best existing accepted documentation, the DD text and the standard textbook definition. The four decisions below were locked by the lead on 2026-10-12, and every other entry follows them.

The worklist is measured, not guessed. §4 — Conformance checks in the Layer 1 audits

The deterministic checks join the Layer 1 review audits in imas_codex/standard_names/review/audits.py, beside run_lexical_lint. That module builds the AuditReport the review pipeline reads (imas_codex/standard_names/review/pipeline.py, where state.audit_report is turned into per-name findings), so these findings reach the reviewer once §5 wires the template, and the harmonize command reads the same report. A second lint module is not built. The generation-path run_audits in imas_codex/standard_names/audits.py, which holds latex_def_check, is synchronous and per-candidate. It is the wrong home, because its findings never reach the reviewer's report. The checks read a name's description and documentation against its selected registry entries and return findings in five classes: a forbidden variant of a registered concept's equation; a non-canonical symbol for a registered quantity; a forbidden locus gloss, such as "separatrix" for plasma_boundary; an opening that does not match the pattern template; and a sign-convention paragraph present or absent against the COCOS rule. In this plan, "the lint" means these checks.

Three of the five classes already have a deterministic owner, and the checks extend it rather than restating it. imas_codex/standard_names/docs_gates.py decides sign-convention prose from the COCOS transformation type (_sign_convention_result), gates the defining equation (_defining_equation_result) and gates symbol definitions (_symbols_are_defined). The registry supplies what those gates lack: the canonical equation form and its forbidden variants, and the canonical symbol per quantity. The template-opening check is added in the same module. The forbidden locus gloss joins the existing convention_violation family that run_lexical_lint emits, not a new class. Today only the hold-out evaluator calls score_documentation, so no review has seen these gates: the audit stage calls it from run_all_audits, and its findings join the AuditReport the review pipeline reads. The registry's forbidden variants are keyed by documentation content and are distinct from the name-decomposition gallery in anti_patterns.yaml.

Some checks a pattern can decide, and those stay pure code. Others need judgment: which form an equation takes when it is written several equivalent ways, whether a gloss equates two loci, which template an opening follows. A regex can only be tuned around those, so they ask Jev one categorising Choice each, through the shared decisions owner of sn-judgment-evaluation §2. They cannot live in review/audits.py, whose contract is deterministic checks runnable without model access. They run as their own asynchronous judge stage in the review pipeline, beside the audit stage, and add their findings to the same per-name list the reviewer receives. §7 builds that stage; this section's done-when covers the deterministic checks only. A Choice is used rather than a narrow Noul because of the 2026-10-12 smoke read (the flux-surface-average smoke read, with its script beside it):

  • a Choice over flux-surface-average forms classified all four boundary entries correctly at probability 1.0, including the carbon entry's wrong dl/B;
  • a Choice over boundary glosses was right on all four;
  • a Noul asking whether the wrong field was used read 0.53 on the defective entry.

Each Jev-backed check is measured on a coordinator-labelled sample through sn-judgment-evaluation §3 before it raises a finding. Until it passes, it reports only.

Done when: on fixtures, the carbon entry's dl/B equation and the argon entry's line-form equation are both flagged, the line form now being a forbidden variant of the locked surface form. The beryllium entry's surface form is not flagged. A boundary gloss reading "separatrix" is flagged. A canonical entry yields zero findings. Each check has a revert-sensitive test. A census over the live WEST roster is recorded with its per-class counts, as the baseline sn-west-prototype-release measures against.

§ Decisions

Where does the conventions registry live?

ISN already carries locus definitions. Concepts, notation and templates exist nowhere. An ISN release adds a dependency to a one-week closure.

Lead ruling 2026-10-12: the registry lives in ISN, beside the vocabularies it is keyed by, and is released before the WEST re-cut. imas-codex holds no copy and reads it through the ISN accessor (editable path source).

Which form defines the flux-surface average?

Both correct forms appear in the catalog. The line form matches transport-code usage; the surface form is coordinate-free. Exactly one is written; the other may be named as equivalent in prose.

Lead ruling 2026-10-12: <f> = ∮ f dS/|∇ψ| / ∮ dS/|∇ψ|. The line form may be named as equivalent in prose and is a forbidden variant as a written equation.

How is the plasma boundary glossed?

24 boundary entries call it the separatrix. That is true only for a diverted plasma.

Lead ruling 2026-10-12: the plasma boundary is the last closed flux surface; the separatrix is named only where a quantity is defined for a diverted configuration.

Which opening does every documentation entry use?

WEST opens in four styles: "The … is …" (77), "This quantity is …" (55), another noun phrase (53) and "<Name> is …" (33).

Lead ruling 2026-10-12: every documentation entry opens with a defining noun phrase of the same shape as its description.
