Plan · standard names · S18 foundation

Measure every judgment before it moves

Most of what the standard-names pipeline spends goes on judging, not writing. Jev judges for about a ten-thousandth of a dollar and, in discovery, judged better than the language model it replaced. Neither fact licenses a move. This plan builds what makes each move a measurement: one call owner, one evaluation module, goldens the incumbent did not write, and a place to store Jev's answers while they are only being watched.

§1 — Why, in numbers

Where the money goes. LLMCost records USD 1,926 of standard-name spend. Review is USD 1,147 of it (60%): review_docs 15,413 calls for USD 848, and review_name 9,053 calls for USD 299. Refine, which is driven by review comments, adds USD 561, so review and refine together are 89%. Generation of names and documentation is USD 217. Since 2026-09-01, review is 77% of spend. Jev costs USD 0.042 per million input tokens with free output. Discovery's judges cost USD 0.00008 to 0.00013 per judgment, against USD 0.033 to 0.055 per incumbent review call, which also runs as a quorum of up to three models.

The incumbent is not ground truth. Measured by the label census of 2026-10-12 (run sn-label-census, reproduced by its review):

  • All 61 names that the WEST binding audit rejects as defective sit at accepted with passing reviews.
  • 1,498 of the 2,027 canonical name reviews of names the catalog later superseded score 3 or 4 out of 4.
  • The incumbent is six models, not one: grok-4.5 8,791 reviews, claude-sonnet-5 6,936, minimax-m3 3,856, qwen3.7-max 3,723, gpt-5.6-luna 2,862, gpt-5.5 2,626.

What discovery learned, and what transfers. These come from discovery-judgments-through-jev §4 and §17, read through run jev-harness-study:

  • A categorise-first Choice with the decision derived in code scored AUC 0.924 [0.886, 0.961], against 0.667 for facet Scores, on source-inspected labels.
  • Goldens written from the text the judge reads were skewed and inconsistent.
  • Repeat calls on a frozen state changed the Choice on 14 of 228 paths.
  • Two operating points were calibrated on the same sample they were reported on.
  • Only one point estimate survived as code: tests/discovery/test_path_category_gate.py asserts the category-gate AUC with sklearn.metrics.roc_auc_score. The interval, the paired difference, the operating point and the stability figures were one-off stdin scripts.

A smoke test on standard-name content, 2026-10-12 (USD 0.00018, four entries):

  • A Choice over flux-surface-average equation forms classified all four boundary entries correctly at probability 1.0, including the carbon entry's wrong dl/B.
  • A Choice over boundary glosses correctly found all four equating the boundary with the separatrix.
  • A narrow Noul for the wrong field read only 0.53 on the defective entry.

The question shape matters as much as the model.

§2 — One decisions owner, and a seat for standard names

The 2026-10-12 code review of the discovery judgment neighbourhood (run review-code-f64de2151701) found the decisions-call contract owned twice. decide_batch in imas_codex/discovery/base/judgment.py owns the fail-soft transport handling and returns the total reported cost, but books it into no tracker. _call_judgment at imas_codex/ids/candidates.py:331 is a single synchronous call that re-implements the fail-soft handling and books the cost into the caller's PipelineCost. Move the booking into the shared owner, which takes an optional cost tracker and step and books the reported cost there. Then delete the wrapper and route its callers through the shared owner, before a third owner appears for standard names. If the synchronous callers need a single-state entry point, it lives inside judgment.py and shares decide_batch's per-call transport handling, not beside it. The two fail-soft rules differ and both are kept: a DecisionsValidationError (a malformed answer) is raised to the single-state caller, as _call_judgment does today and tests/ids/test_candidate_judgment.py asserts, while any other transport fault returns None. decide_batch's own batch behaviour is unchanged. Surface: one seat table is added and the private wrapper is retired. Then add a [tool.imas-codex.sn-judge] seat naming the pinned Jev version. The service tag is not a seat key: it is the runtime service argument of the decisions call, as the candidate stage's JUDGMENT_SERVICE constant is today. Standard names get their own constant, standard-names, passed at every standard-names judge call site, so their Jev spend is attributed separately from discovery's.

The mapping session last touched imas_codex/ids/candidates.py on 2026-10-09. Check live claims on it before dispatch.

Done when: imas_codex/ids/candidates.py defines no private decisions-call wrapper and obtains answers through imas_codex/discovery/base/judgment.py. The tests/ids and tests/discovery suites add no failures against their base. Removing the shared owner's cost-booking line turns the candidate spend assertion red. The sn-judge seat resolves through get_model.

§3 — The evaluation module

imas_codex/discovery/base/evaluation.py, beside the dependency-free judge core, so discovery and standard names share one copy. The metric primitives are sklearn.metrics calls (roc_auc_score, accuracy_score, f1_score, confusion_matrix, cohen_kappa_score), since scikit-learn is already a dependency and the category-gate test already uses it. The module adds what the library lacks: copy-group-aware resampling, the paired difference, an operating point that respects the split, and the stability figures. Surface: one module is added. The hand-rolled pairwise AUC loop in discrimination_metrics (imas_codex/standard_names/benchmark_roles.py) is folded onto the module's AUC, so one AUC owner remains. verdict_flip_quality in the same file measures whether a review flip improved the verdict, which is a different quantity from the option-order flip rate, so it stays. So does the Spearman reviewer agreement in imas_codex/standard_names/benchmark.py. It holds:

  • load_labelled_fixture — rows of unit, label, canonical unit, copy group, label source, source evidence and a fixed calibration/validation split.
  • paired_auc — rank-based, over canonical units, with copy groups kept together.
  • bootstrap_interval — 1,000 resamples of canonical units. It applies to a single arm and to the paired difference between two arms.
  • classification_metrics — accuracy, macro-F1 and a confusion table for a Choice.
  • choose_operating_point — chooses on the calibration split only, against stated false-positive and false-negative costs. It refuses to report a threshold on the split it was chosen on.
  • frozen_state_variation — repeat judgments with an identical state digest, reporting per-field change counts and ranges.
  • option_order_stability — re-asks every Choice with its options permuted, and reports the flip rate. jev-1.13 is documented to lean to the first option.
  • cost_latency_summary — from the reported usage.cost and the wall time of each call.

Done when:

  • Known-answer tests pass: perfectly separating scores give AUC 1.0, reversed scores 0.0, all-tied scores 0.5, and the interval contains its point estimate. Each test fails when its function is reverted to a stub.
  • choose_operating_point raises when asked to report on its calibration split.
  • As a positive control, the existing test_category_gate_reproduces_source_label_auc in tests/discovery/test_path_category_gate.py computes its AUC and interval through the module, over docs/evidence/fragments/discovery-judgments-through-jev/labelled-path-categories.json. It gets 0.9244, with each bound within 0.01 of 0.8858 and 0.9608. That shows the module computes the method discovery used, not a near relative, and no second copy of the control is written.
  • discrimination_metrics returns the same AUC as before through the module, and its existing tests pass unchanged.

§5 — The goldens nobody has labelled yet

B — documentation acceptable cannot be assembled from what exists. Fewer than 300 independent items carry a docs label, almost all negative, against 16,282 incumbent docs-axis review records (the 15,413 review_docs calls of §1, each writing one or more records). Any docs-axis claim made before this golden exists would be the incumbent grading itself.

The coordinator labels 400 entries, stratified by domain and pattern family and balanced within 35% to 65%. Each is read against its DD source and the house-style registry, with a written rubric. The lead independently labels a 50-item overlap and adjudicates the disagreements. The same procedure supplies the synonym side of golden D: the coordinator pre-screens candidate pairs by shared unit and coordinate, and the lead rules on the shortlist.

This is labelling by a person who can read the DD, and it is deliberately not dispatched to a model. A model-labelled golden would measure agreement between models.

Done when: golden B holds 400 labels within the balance bounds. The coordinator-lead agreement (Cohen's kappa) on the 50-item overlap is recorded, with the disagreements and their rulings. Golden D holds at least 40 lead-ruled synonym pairs.

§6 — A shadow store, read by no gate

Jev answers are stored beside the incumbent's, in the convention the facility schema already uses: each value X, its distribution X_probs, its X_confidence, and the judgment_model that produced it. Storing the model means re-judging after a model change is a string comparison, as in discovery's claim predicates.

The records are a distinct judge kind on the review record, extending _build_review_record in imas_codex/standard_names/review/pipeline.py. Until a deployment decision is made under §7, no accept, refine or export path reads them. That lets shadow comparisons accumulate on live traffic without changing a single outcome. Surface: the review record gains the shadow fields and nothing is retired, because no existing field carries a judge's distribution or the model that produced it. The judgment_model property follows the convention discovery already writes (imas_codex/discovery/paths/scorer.py).

Done when: the schema declares the fields and the generated models carry them. A shadow write round-trips. A test shows that the accept and refine paths return identical decisions with shadow records present and absent, and the test fails when one gate is pointed at a shadow field.

§7 — The rule a judge must pass to decide anything

A Jev judge replaces an incumbent decision only when all four hold:

  • On the golden's validation split, the lower 95% bound of the paired AUC difference (Jev minus incumbent) is above the margin locked below. The incumbent is reported per model and as the quorum, since it is six models.
  • Its operating point was chosen on the calibration split.
  • Its option-order flip rate and frozen-state variation are reported and inside bounds the prototype states.
  • Its cost per decision is lower.

A judge that falls short is not dropped. On 2026-10-12 the lead ruled that a shortfall signals the design needs work, not that Jev should be abandoned. The judge stays in shadow mode while its design is iterated: the state it is given, the question shape and criteria, the templates, and the context retrieved and reranked for it (itself a Jev rerank). Each iteration records what changed and its re-measured interval, so the path to passing is visible. Ordering surfaces — queues, candidate lists, comparators — may adopt a Jev rerank without passing the rule, because a rerank that keeps the incumbent order on failure can never be worse than it.

§ Decisions

How far below the incumbent may a Jev judge's AUC fall and still take the decision?

The gate compares the lower 95% bound of the paired difference with this margin. A cheaper judge that is no worse is a win, but "no worse" needs a number.

Lead ruling 2026-10-12: −0.02 AUC at the 95% bound. The lead expects Jev to be better on experience so far. A judge that falls short is a signal that the design needs work — prompt, templates, context search and Jev reranking of that context — not a reason to drop the judge. It stays in shadow and is iterated and re-measured.

What Jev spend is authorised for standard-name prototypes and shadow runs?

Discovery held each run under USD 3 and spent cents. A shadow pass over all 5,130 names at five questions each is about USD 1 to 2.

Lead ruling 2026-10-12: USD 10 of Jev spend for the sprint's standard-name prototypes and shadow runs, each run reporting against it.
