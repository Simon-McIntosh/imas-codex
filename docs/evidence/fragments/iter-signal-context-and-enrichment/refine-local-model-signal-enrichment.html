Batch-size changes retain all shared retrieval text and each selected signal’s complete group/signal context, removing only other signal blocks and renumbering output indices. Claim order differs from rendered group order; the replay resolves the latter from the frozen accessors, including two valid empty accessors. Splits retain the original ten-signal context boundaries: the size-three arm makes 80 requests (three triples and one single in each of twenty frozen contexts), rather than mixing different contexts into 67 new requests. A preflight checked 800 target contexts across the four batch sizes before smaller-batch generation. The final audit repeats the equality check for every target in every arm.

The fitted prompt retains the entire baseline task and adds full field-local enumerations, one schema-valid worked example and the output schema last. Reasoning alone uses a larger completion bound. The re-ask quotes the actual Pydantic validation error and makes at most one replacement request. Raw parsing always validates the untouched first response; fences and recoverable wrappers remain raw failures. Delivery counts descriptions emitted by the production worker to the private sink. Validation-directed re-asks and existing provider-abort retries are counted separately; both remain within a total bound of two requests where replacement is enabled. A schema-valid response can still omit indices, so raw parsing and signal coverage have different denominators.

What changed and the constraint control

The fitted prompt alone raised raw parsing from 0% to 80% and delivered 151/200. Batches of five delivered 129/200, three 185/200, and one 197/200; smaller batches alone retained low raw parse rates. One strict validation re-ask alone delivered 190/200. Thinking with a 32,768-token bound delivered 120/200 in 1,647.40 seconds, below both non-thinking baselines. Combining the fitted prompt and re-ask with batches of three reached 95% raw parsing and 197/200 delivery. The single-signal combination reached 98.5% raw parsing and 200/200 delivery: 197 on the first call and three after one re-ask, with no ordinary retries. It adds three descriptions and 3.5 percentage points of raw parsing over the three-signal combination for 106.68 extra seconds per 200 signals; input tokens rise from 1,674,325 to 4,015,551.

Removing JSON-object mode raised raw parsing from 61/80 (76.25%) to 76/80 (95%) for batches of three, an 18.75 percentage-point increase, and from 177/200 (88.5%) to 197/200 (98.5%) for single-signal calls, a 10 percentage-point increase. Delivery rose from 179 to 197 and from 196 to 200 respectively. The requested expectation that constraint removal lowers raw parsing is falsified in both controlled pairs. The selected best configuration therefore uses no forced decoding; reliable full-schema decoding is not claimed.

The archived-defect reproduction printed ARCHIVED_REPRODUCTION {"responses": 20, "strict": 0, "delivered": 130, "paid": 200}. The fresh baselines then reproduced the same zero raw parses and 130–131 delivered descriptions. The pre-change transport probe printed SCHEMA_FORWARDING_REPRODUCTION requested True forwarded False, confirming the forwarding defect before opt-in repair. The route’s own serving source establishes both JSON-schema and JSON-object response formats. An adversarial enum control demanded a forbidden answer but returned only the schema’s permitted marker with thinking off and on; the latter produced reasoning text and twelve reported reasoning tokens. This proves that the forwarding path can constrain a small schema. The full enrichment schema nevertheless aborts on all twenty real batches. Raw scores were checked against a known valid response, malformed text and fenced JSON before a uniform zero was accepted.
