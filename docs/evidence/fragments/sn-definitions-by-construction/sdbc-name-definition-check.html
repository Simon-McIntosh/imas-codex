A review check that a name and its definition say the same thing

Measure. On the held-out two thirds of §4's labelled names the delivered check — a deterministic first pass and a typed judgment read by the review seat, either of which flags — finds 28 of 47 names labelled as mismatched (59.6 % recall) and flags 27 of 207 names labelled as agreeing (13.0 % false-flag rate). The recall floor of 80 % is not met and the false-flag ceiling of 10 % is exceeded by the union. The deterministic pass alone finds 14 of 47 at 11 of 207 and the judgment alone 16 of 47 at 17 of 207. Counts are from name-definition-check-results.json. The lead ruling of 2026-11-13 accepted the chat review seat at its own measured reading and made the 80 % figure a target for a successor rather than a bar; the seat's score and its pass over every published name are recorded below.

What was built

One entry in the named review taxonomy, definition_states_qualifier_name_lacks (axis docs, dimension physics_accuracy), decided deterministically by name_definition_agreement_check in imas_codex/standard_names/audits.py and carried into the review report by run_name_definition_checks. It adds no new gate or command: its refusal is the docs review's existing rejection, and a flagged name rejects review unless a recorded reason answers it. It calls no model.

The check reads the definition and the name against each other, one qualifier family at a time — equivalent-electron counting, an aggregation (flux-surface or volume average), an integration (volume, surface, wall), a normalisation, a relative reference, a vector stated for a scalar, a restriction to one face, one coil instance, one module, one reaction branch, one instrument class or one discharge phase, and an explicitly unspecified umbrella. A family fires only when the definition's wording adds the qualifier and the name carries none of the tokens that would state it. The description is read as the definition; the documentation is read only for the two families whose wording cannot describe a neighbouring quantity.

The labels, and how they stayed held out

The labels are §4's, produced by one reader reading name, description, documentation and unit together — not by this check, not by the pipeline judge, and not by any model that runs the check ("one reader, reading name, description, documentation and unit together; no pipeline judge, review check or decide_batch produced any label"). A second, independent reader labelled 60 of the same names and agrees exactly on class under the lead's species ruling for 45 of 60 (75 %).

The 381 labelled names were split once, by committed seed 7714, stratified by class under the ruling: 127 development and 254 held-out. The rule set was shaped on the development third only; the held-out rows' labels were not read until the rules were frozen. On the development third the check reaches 21 of 24 (87.5 %) recall at 7 of 103 (6.8 %) false flags — the figure the shaping produced, and the figure the held-out arm refutes.

Why the held-out arm fails, and what it says

The development third was not a smaller copy of the sample. Of its 24 mismatched names, 5 were the equivalent-electron family and 6 the umbrella family, and the lexical families the check reads carried 21 of them. The held-out two thirds concentrate in the two families a lexical rule reads worst: 12 of the 47 are umbrella parents and 5 are definitions narrowed to one object, against 1 equivalent-electron case (see the mismatched_missed list in the results file). The check's precision transfers (5.3 % false flags on held-out against 6.8 % on development); its recall does not.

The honest reading is that a lexical check over the description alone cannot carry the umbrella-parent and narrowed-object classes: those two families are 17 of the 47 held-out mismatches and the rules recognise them only when the definition uses the catalog's own umbrella wording. Reaching 80 % needs a read of what the definition quantifies, which is the judgment the pipeline's review seat exists to make. That judgment was built and is measured below; it carries the umbrella class the lexical rule cannot and still misses the floor, so the node is filed blocked rather than closed.

Abstract parents, from the pipeline's state rather than from prose

The catalog files carry no parent flag, so counting from the definition wording undercounts badly: only three published definitions declare an umbrella explicitly, one stating one quantity and two stating no single quantity. The pipeline's own state is the instrument — read from HAS_PARENT, 546 of 1,975 published names are derived parents (they carry at least one accepted child), and 107 of the 381 labelled names are — which is why an umbrella is recognised from the graph's state rather than from any wording in its own definition. The judgment finds 9 of the 13 names §4 labelled as umbrellas. Whether each of the 546 is published with a definition that fixes one quantity is the adjudication the blocked manifest asks the lead to make; the node may not settle it.

Readers disagreeing

The 15 names the two readers disagree on are scored separately on the first reader's ruling column. The check flags 2 of them — current_density_due_to_bootstrap_current_drive (umbrella) and pulse_duration (restricted to the confined-plasma phase).

The refusal, shown

tests/standard_names/test_name_definition_agreement.py plants a normalised quantity under an unnormalised name and runs the docs review with every rubric in its accepted category and every claim supported: the review rejects, and the only check that stops failing when the same name states the normalisation is this one. The negative control mutates the check so the planted normalisation is not read; two tests fail and the gate log records EXIT=1.

The judgment, and the second scoring of the held-out third

The section's decision was to make this a judgment rather than a lexical rule, so the taxonomy now carries a second entry beside the deterministic one: definition_qualifier_absent_from_name, a typed Choice read by the configured review seat (sn-judge) over the name with its grammar parse and segments, the one-line description, the documentation, the unit and the pipeline's own derived-parent evidence (derived_children, read from HAS_PARENT). Its options are the six forms the definition can add plus agrees. A name is flagged by the delivered check when the cheap deterministic pass finds a qualifier or the judgment answers anything but agrees.

The held-out third was scored twice, deliberately. The first scoring measured the frozen lexical check and is what showed a lexical rule cannot carry the umbrella and narrowed-object classes. The lead then directed that the check become a judgment, so the same held-out rows were scored a second time — against a judgment shaped on the development third only, frozen before that second scoring, with no held-out row read while the question was written. The second scoring is therefore a genuine hold-out result for the judgment, and not a tuning set: the judgment's question text was never changed after the first held-out reading.

The chat review seat, scored on the names both readers labelled alike

The instrument is a chat review seat, not the lexical and judgment union. It is one of the named checks a name already passes through: the docs review calls judge_name_definitions in imas_codex/standard_names/review/name_definition_seat.py, and a flagged verdict becomes a review hold raised by name-definition-seat beside the registry-conformance, banned-prose and contradiction holds. An unanswered call holds the candidate rather than accepting it. It calls the configured docs review seat through the shared call layer — get_sn_review_docs_models()[0], which is openrouter/anthropic/claude-sonnet-5 — and reads the name, its description, its documentation, its unit and the child names where the graph records any.

What the seat does read well is the class: 17 restrictions to one object, 13 counting conventions, 11 families of quantities, 9 integrations, 3 weightings, 3 normalisations across its 56 flags. Its 15 false flags are concentrated in names whose unit or locus makes the definition look narrowed — plasma_temperature, vertical_electron_velocity, iron_density, total_ion_particle_source_rate — which is the same boundary the two readers disputed. No catalog run was made, because the ruling gates it on the bars holding.

Cost. USD 2.2711 for the 313-name score — about USD 0.0073 per name, which projects to about USD 14 for all 1,975 published names, inside the USD 25 ceiling. Spend to date on this node is USD 0.045 for the earlier judgment seat plus this USD 2.2711.

The names the two readers disagree on

The judgment was also run on the 15 names where the two readers disagree, scored on the first reader's ruling column. That column calls 4 of the 15 mismatched and the judgment finds 2 of those 4 (density_at_magnetic_axis, momentum); it misses pulse_duration, which the deterministic pass catches, and particle_density. Altogether the judgment flags 6 of 15, answering six family-of-quantities and nine agrees, and the delivered union flags 7 of 15 (3 of the 4 the first reader calls mismatched). Per-name verdicts are in readers_disagree in the results file.

The four extra flags are the interesting half. Each is a name the first reader called agreeing and the second reader called mismatched, so on those the judgment sides with the dissent — straight_field_line_angle, current_density_due_to_bootstrap_current_drive, bulk_neutral_internal_state_velocity and fast_ion_charge_state_power_density. Of the two names the first reader alone calls umbrellas, momentum agrees with it and density_at_magnetic_axis does too, while the second reader called the latter agreeing and the former a unit mismatch. Fifteen adversarial names cannot settle which reader is right; what they show is that the judgment is not anchored to either, and that the first reader's ruling column is what the held-out recall figure is scored against.

The seat over every published name, and what it proposes

The lead ruling of 2026-11-13 accepted the seat at its measured reading and made the 80 % figure a target for a successor, so the seat ran over every published name. 1,977 published names were read from the live graph read-only — a name is published when it carries the catalog export commit (catalog_commit_sha); the graph's status field marks drafts and superseded names, not the cut — with each name's definition, documentation, unit and child names. All 1,977 were judged and none went unanswered. The pass is resumable: each batch's verdicts append to a GPFS JSONL as they land, so a run cut short keeps what it paid for and a rerun judges only the names not yet recorded.
