A review check that a name and its definition say the same thing

Measure. On the held-out two thirds of §4's labelled names the check finds 14 of 47 names labelled as mismatched (29.8 % recall) and flags 11 of 207 names labelled as agreeing (5.3 % false-flag rate). The recall floor of 80 % is not met; the false-flag ceiling of 10 % is met. Counts are from name-definition-check-results.json.

What was built

One entry in the named review taxonomy, definition_states_qualifier_name_lacks (axis docs, dimension physics_accuracy), decided deterministically by name_definition_agreement_check in imas_codex/standard_names/audits.py and carried into the review report by run_name_definition_checks. It adds no new gate or command: its refusal is the docs review's existing rejection, and a flagged name rejects review unless a recorded reason answers it. It calls no model.

The check reads the definition and the name against each other, one qualifier family at a time — equivalent-electron counting, an aggregation (flux-surface or volume average), an integration (volume, surface, wall), a normalisation, a relative reference, a vector stated for a scalar, a restriction to one face, one coil instance, one module, one reaction branch, one instrument class or one discharge phase, and an explicitly unspecified umbrella. A family fires only when the definition's wording adds the qualifier and the name carries none of the tokens that would state it. The description is read as the definition; the documentation is read only for the two families whose wording cannot describe a neighbouring quantity.

The labels, and how they stayed held out

The labels are §4's, produced by one reader reading name, description, documentation and unit together — not by this check, not by the pipeline judge, and not by any model that runs the check ("one reader, reading name, description, documentation and unit together; no pipeline judge, review check or decide_batch produced any label"). A second, independent reader labelled 60 of the same names and agrees exactly on class under the lead's species ruling for 45 of 60 (75 %).

The 381 labelled names were split once, by committed seed 7714, stratified by class under the ruling: 127 development and 254 held-out. The rule set was shaped on the development third only; the held-out rows' labels were not read until the rules were frozen. On the development third the check reaches 21 of 24 (87.5 %) recall at 7 of 103 (6.8 %) false flags — the figure the shaping produced, and the figure the held-out arm refutes.

Why the held-out arm fails, and what it says

The development third was not a smaller copy of the sample. Of its 24 mismatched names, 5 were the equivalent-electron family and 6 the umbrella family, and the lexical families the check reads carried 21 of them. The held-out two thirds concentrate in the two families a lexical rule reads worst: 12 of the 47 are umbrella parents and 5 are definitions narrowed to one object, against 1 equivalent-electron case (see the mismatched_missed list in the results file). The check's precision transfers (5.3 % false flags on held-out against 6.8 % on development); its recall does not.

The honest reading is that a lexical check over the description alone cannot carry the umbrella-parent and narrowed-object classes: those two families are 17 of the 47 held-out mismatches and the rules recognise them only when the definition uses the catalog's own umbrella wording. Reaching 80 % needs a read of what the definition quantifies, which is the judgment the pipeline's review seat exists to make. This node delivers the working deterministic check, its taxonomy entry and its refusal test, and records the measure that says a deterministic rule is not sufficient. Turning the entry into a Jev question is the next decision, and it belongs to the coordinator.

Abstract parents

The catalog files carry no parent flag, so the census reads the definition wording. Three published definitions declare an umbrella explicitly: one states one quantity and two state no single quantity (abstract_parent_census in the results file). Those two are the section's defect: an abstract parent published with a definition that fixes no single quantity. The other 19 umbrella names §4 labelled are not published with that wording, which is why the lexical check misses them.

Readers disagreeing

The 15 names the two readers disagree on are scored separately on the first reader's ruling column. The check flags 2 of them — current_density_due_to_bootstrap_current_drive (umbrella) and pulse_duration (restricted to the confined-plasma phase).

The refusal, shown

tests/standard_names/test_name_definition_agreement.py plants a normalised quantity under an unnormalised name and runs the docs review with every rubric in its accepted category and every claim supported: the review rejects, and the only check that stops failing when the same name states the normalisation is this one. The negative control mutates the check so the planted normalisation is not read; two tests fail and the gate log records EXIT=1.
