Shared definition parts rendered from one stored wording

A definition has parts that belong to a concept shared with other names — the operator applied, the population counted, the locus — and parts that belong to this name alone. The writer now emits only the latter. Every shared part is substituted from one stored wording, and a definition that departs from it after rendering is refused before it is stored.

The mechanism

PartStored wordingWhere it is rendered
Operatoroperator_definition_sentence: the grammar library's operator entry, sentence and equation, unchanged for every name that applies itdocs_gates.shared_part_sentences, installed by _documentation_with_registry_form
PopulationSPECIES_POPULATION_SENTENCE: The density counts ions summed over all charge states; neutral atoms are excluded. — the catalog decision of 2026-11-12same renderer, on every bare species density
Locuslocus_definition_sentence: the locus registry's definition with its equation and symbolssame renderer, unchanged by this section

The renderer removes a written sentence that states a shared part in other words and installs the stored sentence, so a definition carries one wording per part rather than two. shared_part_findings reports a definition that still carries a paraphrase or omits the part, as a further class of the documentation conformance findings (shared_part_altered), and _refuse_altered_shared_parts refuses such a definition in the writer before persist_generated_docs runs.

Nothing was written to the project graph. The median is lower, and the comparison cannot be read as a quality drop. Written median 0.850 over fifteen names against a published reviewer_score_docs median of 0.908 over the eleven that exist live. The written names were reviewed by the Jev-deciding cascade, which records the acceptance floor (0.85 exactly, from DEFAULT_MIN_SCORE) when a decision passes and grades only the four names it routed to the chat reviewer; the published scores are quorum means from the graded reviewer chain. Nine of the fifteen written names carry the floor, so the medians measure two scoring regimes rather than two qualities. A like-for-like comparison needs one regime on both sides, and this node does not have it.

Wording proposed to the grammar library

The species population rule has no sentence in the grammar library's concepts.yml. It is stored in this repository as docs_gates.SPECIES_POPULATION_SENTENCE and proposed upstream as a population entry so that one stored wording serves every species name.
