The reverse gap is as sharp. The labels record one binary verdict with one first-failing check; the score returns continuous probabilities for three separate dimensions, one probability per asked check, and a claim relation. No label row records a claim at all — 0 of the 216 calibration rows carry one — so the 0.2 weight the claim channel holds is judged by nothing in the fixture.

3. What the claim state carries

Most claims say nothing, and the DD text is why. The mean share of claims that say nothing is 0.687 (median 0.750); 68 of 216 entries have every claim silent, 68 of 216 are over 90% silent, and only 56 carry evidence in half their claims. The frozen DD source text is a median of 22 characters, with 65 of 216 entries under eight characters — a bare Value or a unit string. The enriched description is a median of 163 characters. The share of silent claims and the DD text length move together at Pearson -0.617.

The claim state carries four fields only: documentation, documentation_claims, description and dd_source_docs. Across the 216 calibration entries every one is unobserved on the four fields a claim could be checked against — units 216, links 216, related neighbours 216, source paths 216. Claims per entry range 1 to 11 (median 7), so the channel is neither absent nor thin in count — it is starved of the evidence a relation could be checked against.

4. Does the same gap explain the incumbent at 0.514?

On calibration the incumbent scores 0.603 [0.519, 0.678] over 210 entries. It is near-constant: 0.562 to 1.000, only 31 distinct values. A near-constant score has AUC near 0.5 almost by construction, because most of the pairing is ties, so its own flatness is a sufficient explanation of its near-chance result and needs no appeal to what it reads. Whether the same label-versus-question gap applies cannot be settled from the frozen answers: two arms failing for a shared reason and two arms failing for unrelated reasons look the same here, and they are statistically indistinguishable (31 distinct values against 205, overlapping intervals). The label agreement field does not separate them either: only 29 of 216 entries carry lead_overlap, 9 accepted and 20 not, and the incumbent mean over those 29 is 0.929 against 0.930 overall. The most this fixture supports is the part that is shared and measurable: both arms reduce an entry to one scalar, and that scalar is not recorded against the link and house-style properties the labels fail on.

The best calibration-only change is drop the claim term, inverted and flat checks: 0.657 [0.575, 0.743] against the baseline 0.610. It costs nothing, because it is a reweighting of answers already frozen. The ranked changes that need new calls are the two structural repairs: putting the 15 never-asked checks in front of the judge, and stocking the claim state with the evidence it currently lacks. Both raise prompt size rather than adding a provider, so both keep the local judge.

Baseline judge cost, for scale: USD 1.61e-05 per claim and 0.090 s per claim, 0.000110 USD and 0.609 s per entry on the base judgments.

Verdict. The score sits near chance on calibration for two reasons that compound: two checks are inverted and one is constant, so the named-check mean carries a cancellation; and the claim channel, worth 0.2 of the aggregate, says nothing on most entries because the DD text it is given is a few characters long. The labels fail on link and house-style properties that the judge is never asked about at all. A reweighting recovers part of the gap with no new calls; the larger share needs the never-asked checks and a stocked claim state.
