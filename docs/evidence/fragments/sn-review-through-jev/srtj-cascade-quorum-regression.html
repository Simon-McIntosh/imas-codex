Shadow routes no longer displace the incumbent's rows

The shadow cascade router changed the incumbent review path's observable behaviour in two ways. The route row was persisted before the incumbent ran, so the first write_reviews call carried the judge_kind=jev shadow row and the quorum's own rows arrived second; and a route row was written even when the incumbent produced no reviews at all, so a failed cycle left a row describing a candidate nothing had reviewed. A third defect surfaced in the same reproduction: a check record whose failure_probability was null was compared directly against its cutoff.

Reproduced before the repair at 1197a5b05, which carries the merged router: tests/standard_names/test_review_quorum_pool.py ran 9 failed and 2 passed at EXIT=1, and the declared negative control at f69054241 fails the same 9 ids at EXIT=1. The first assertion to fail read TypeError: '>' not supported between instances of 'NoneType' and 'float' on the shadow row's null score, and the captured call list showed the shadow row at write_reviews_calls[0] with failure_reason "ValueError: no usable judgment for name review".

The route row is now stored after the incumbent returns: the quorum runner persists its own records through a wrapper that persists records only for a cycle chain that produced reviews, and the router stores its shadow row beside them. An incumbent that returns None writes nothing and returns None, on both the judged and unjudged paths. A null failure_probability names no failure rather than a comparison, so the record routes on its other checks and its score.

Two tests in tests/standard_names/test_review_cascade.py show the ordering against the real store. test_shadow_row_is_written_after_the_incumbents_rows drives an incumbent that persists two incumbent rows and asserts the recorded call lengths are [2, 1] with an incumbent judge_kind on the first call and a resolution_role=shadow row on the second. test_failed_incumbent_cycle_writes_no_shadow_row drives an incumbent that produced no reviews and asserts it returns None with no write call at all. Overlaid on f69054241 both are red at EXIT=1: the first reports assert [1, 2] == [2, 1], the second reports the shadow row it should not have written. Both pass at head.
