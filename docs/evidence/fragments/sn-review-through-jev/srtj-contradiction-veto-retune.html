What changed

All of it in imas_codex/standard_names/review/jev_review.py. Two decisions are now made in code, both properties of the two texts rather than of the judge's reading. label_only_source is true when a DD source's every word belongs to the vocabulary of field placement, so the text names no quantity. comparison_sources drops those sources and build_docs_state records the remainder as the claim's evidence, unobserved when none is left. disclaims_locus_or_aggregation is true when a claim denies fixing a locus or an aggregation — "The quantity has no mandatory locus or aggregation", "It does not prescribe a wavenumber sum or time or flux-surface average" — and source_specialises is true when the evidence names one instance of the quantity. When both hold, claim_determination returns "says nothing", and settled_claim_answers applies that at the aggregation boundary: the claim cannot veto the entry and it is excluded from the claim-support mean, because there is no denial to report. The question text is back to its base form. Stating the specialisation rule to the judge cost recall without removing the false firings, so the rule is applied deterministically instead. claim_contradicts and CONTRADICTION_VETO_PROBABILITY are unchanged, and nothing implicates the threshold.

A corrected harness, and what it corrects

The first run of this replay measured the base question's recall as 60 of 60 and the rewording's as 32 of 60. Both numbers were wrong. The question table was built from the first row's claim list, so any row with more claims than that row had was asked only about the truncated indices and scored as not fired. The table is now built over placeholder claims, which is possible because a claim question depends only on its index, not on the claim's words. The numbers reported above are from the corrected harness, and the earlier 32 is not a measurement of the rewording's recall.
