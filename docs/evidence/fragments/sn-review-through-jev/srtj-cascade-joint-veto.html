Choosing the veto checks jointly instead of one at a time

The defect. The route rule refines an item when any selected check crosses its cutoff. Each cutoff had been fitted alone, allowing it to flag at most a tenth of the accepted items; nothing bounded what several such cutoffs flag together. The cutoffs compounded, so the router vetoed most of the accepted population: on the name axis it accepted 22 of 363 calibration items and refined 274, on documentation it accepted 25 of 216 and refined 151. The score itself ranks well; the decision built on it was never validated as a decision.

What changed. A calibration module rebuilds both axes from the frozen receipts. Checks are now added to the veto set one at a time by the defect recall they add, and the union of the chosen cutoffs is held to at most a tenth of the accepted calibration items on each axis. A check left out is not a veto; it still feeds the score. Accept and reject cutoffs are re-derived by the existing policy.

Control. The rebuild reproduces the cutoffs it replaces where the two procedures should agree. The name accept cutoff comes out at 0.7085 against the recorded 0.7097, and the documentation accept cutoff at 0.5541 against the recorded 0.5500. The score is untouched: validation AUC is 0.745246 for names and 0.752580 for documentation, the values the earlier arms measured. Neither receipt, nor any judgment, is re-fetched, and no model is called, so the run costs nothing.

Validation split, before and after

The calibration split is what the cutoffs are fitted on; the validation split is measured with the same cutoffs and never read while fitting. False-veto rate is the share of accepted items the union of cutoffs routes to refine — the quantity the budget bounds. Refine precision is the share of refined items that are planted defects; accept precision is the share of accepted items that are defensible.

Refining less is not achieved by refining worse. On the calibration split the name axis catches 74 of 223 defects while flagging 14 of 140 accepted items, and refine precision rises from 0.679 to 0.841; documentation catches 82 of 144 defects while flagging 6 of 72 accepted items, with refine precision rising from 0.815 to 0.932. On validation the reductions hold — refine share 0.814 to 0.352 for names, 0.647 to 0.391 for documentation.

Checks dropped from the veto

These checks still feed the score; they are no longer vetoes. The name axis keeps one of its nineteen cutoffs and drops the other eighteen (advisory_alias_collapsed, ambiguity_in_isolation, bare_shape_parameter_missing_surface, bound_to_wrong_object, coupled_vs_launched_confusion, device_attributed_observable, dropped_distinguishing_axis, malformed_physical_base, model_surname_suffix, non_canonical_locus_token, non_canonical_operator_join, ordered_sample_positional_token, over_qualified_modifier, over_specialised_parent, source_fidelity_unsupported_qualifier, unit_kind_inconsistent, unregistered_token_in_closed_segment, value_provenance_in_name), keeping missing_required_segment at 0.62. The documentation axis keeps four (defining_equation_missing, description_false_of_some_binding, description_template_opening_mismatch, sign_convention_missing), adds raw_dd_reference_in_prose and retains the hard contradiction veto claim_contradicts_source, and drops eight (description_not_single_definition, equation_physically_incorrect, false_equivalence_with_related, incompatible_physics_qualifier, latex_escaping_broken, non_normative_documentation). The contradiction veto is a fixed rule rather than a fitted cutoff and is kept only because the union still sits inside the budget with it.

The per-check admissibility bar is what keeps the veto sets small: a check is only a candidate if some cutoff of it flags at most a tenth of the accepted items, catches at least two defects and separates the two populations by at least 0.02. On the name axis one check clears that bar, so the union budget binds at exactly 0.100 — a further check would cross it.

Where this does not hold

The budget is fitted on the calibration split, and it does not transfer: on validation the false-veto rate is 0.183 for names and 0.189 for documentation, both above the tenth the calibration split holds. The name accept precision also falls from 0.875 to 0.750 on validation, so the acceptance decision is the weaker of the two out of sample. The cutoffs are therefore evidence that the decision rule can be budgeted and that refining less does not mean refining worse — not evidence that either bound holds on unseen items. A held-out budget, or a budget fitted on the tighter of the two splits, is unresolved work.

Negative control. Replacing joint selection with per-check selection fails test_joint_selection_keeps_the_union_inside_its_budget on a fixture of four checks whose flagged shares are disjoint: each flags 0.09 of the accepted items, admissible alone; their union flags 0.36. The three remaining tests — the fixture's own validity, determinism of the rebuild, and the missing-receipt refusal — pass in both arms, so the failure is attributable to the selection rule alone.
