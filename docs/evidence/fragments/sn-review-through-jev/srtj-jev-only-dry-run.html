The Jev-only dry run over the fixed cohort: the docs axis slips its accept-rate check, and its unjudged share is a provider failure rather than a decision

The cascade was run as the deciding route over the fixed cohort on both axes, in scratch graphs, with nothing switched in production. The name axis decided 230 of 231 items and the documentation axis 143 of 219. Neither axis clears the accept-rate check against its matched calibration target: the name axis accepts far more than the panel did at the matched cutoff (0.8874 against a 0.6148 target), and the docs axis accepts far fewer (0.5662 against 0.9905). The name axis clears the undecided check; the docs axis does not, and its 76 undecided items are almost entirely items for which no Jev judgment could be obtained at all — a backend failure recorded as a missing row, not a route the cascade chose. The paid-panel audit wrote 13 rows across the two axes and recorded 5 skips. Every audit row stayed out of the review aggregates on both axes, and total spend, USD 2.2040, is inside the USD 3.31 envelope.

These are service failures on the judging model, not a routing policy the cascade applied, and they are what pushes the docs axis past the 2 percent bound.

The paid-panel audit

The audit sample ran as configured, at the 5 percent rate, and where its budget or its lease refused it a row was still written carrying the reason. Names: 8 rows written across 231 items, 1 a skip (panel produced no verdict), 7 paired, 5 agreeing — an agreement rate of 0.7143. On those 7 paired items the panel accepted all 7 and Jev accepted 5 and refined 2. Documentation: 5 rows written across 219 items, 4 skips (3 panel produced no verdict, 1 review audit lease refused), 1 paired, and that pair disagrees — the panel accepted where Jev refined. The audit is too thin on documentation to say anything about drift there.

No audit row was read as a route on either axis: the runner partitions judge rows by resolution_role before it chooses the row a route is read from, and reports audit_rows_read_as_route as empty on both. Every audit row was also excluded from the review aggregates, which is the check recorded below.

Containment

Each axis ran against its own scratch Neo4j and nothing in production was touched. The runner reads the run rows and the cost rows on both the scratch instance and the live endpoint after each piece: name — 1 run row and 247 cost rows on the scratch URI bolt://127.0.0.1:38027, 0 of each on the live endpoint bolt://98dci4-gpu-0002:7687; documentation — 1 run row and 323 cost rows on bolt://127.0.0.1:40717, 0 of each live. Both verdicts are pass, and both scratch instances were stopped and their trees removed (scratch_removed.exists_after false for both).

What this does not show

Both pieces are recorded failed by the runner's own completion guard, and the two failures are different in kind. The name piece refuses on transient_scope_residue: four names were left with stale claims in the scratch scope, which is residue of the same reviewer failures that produced the fallbacks, and it does not bear on the routes measured. The documentation piece refuses because 101 selected items were left pending — the strict form of the accounting above, which counts an item the guard could not close. Both pieces hold their full census regardless, and both logs end in a real EXIT=1; the censuses in this fragment are the pieces' own numbers, not the guard's.

The documentation axis cannot support a claim about Jev's operating point. A third of its items never reached a judgment, the misses are spread across the cohort rather than confined to a corner of it, and the accept rate is computed only over the 143 items that did decide. A re-run on a lane where the judging model answers would be necessary before the docs accept rate means what the name accept rate means here. The name axis is the more nearly complete measurement of the two and is the one worth reading.

Nothing was switched. Both runs set [sn-review].cascade-deciding to true in process only, recorded on each piece as switch_value: true, against scratch graphs that were removed afterwards. Whether to switch the cascade on in production is the coordinator's call, not this node's.
