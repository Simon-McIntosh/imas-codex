Every disposable Neo4j starter now goes through the shared fixture

Outcome

Three standard-names test files still launched their own Neo4j, each choosing two ports with a local _free_port() called twice in a row — the same-port hazard _free_port_pair exists to remove. test_review_holds.py, test_bare_doc_link_normalize.py and test_source_refresh_docs_requeue.py now take the package’s disposable_neo4j_empty fixture; their local launchers and the imports only they used are deleted. grep -rn "_free_port()" tests/standard_names/ returns nothing: the only port chooser left in the package is _free_port_pair in conftest.py.

The pair routine itself had no test, so a revert to two sequential single-port calls would have been silent. A committed test now models the kernel’s behaviour that makes the two forms differ: a port becomes free again when its socket closes, so a sequential pair collides in the model while a pair held open at once does not.

The pair test, and what it would catch

test_the_shared_fixture_asks_for_two_distinct_ports replaces the socket module in the conftest namespace with a fake pool. bind allocates the lowest free port in the pool and keeps it allocated until close, so the pool hands the same number back exactly when the previous holder has closed. The test first drives two single-port cycles itself and requires them to collide — without that, a distinct pair would prove nothing about the fixture’s order of operations, because the fake might be handing out distinct ports regardless. It then calls _free_port_pair() through the patched module and requires the two ports that come back to differ.

The mutation was applied to conftest.py in the worktree, its run captured to the negative-control log, and the file restored byte-for-byte (git diff --stat on the file shows nothing afterwards).

The one head error, and why it is not a test outcome

Head arm a2 recorded ERROR at teardown of test_the_real_writer_stores_the_audit_row_and_the_aggregate_ignores_it: Failed: Timeout (>30.0s) from pytest-timeout, raised inside disposable_neo4j_uri’s directory.cleanup() → shutil.rmtree. It is the scratch store being removed, not an assertion. The same cleanup of the same test took 15.01 s in the base arm (base a1), and head arm a3 completed the identical selection with EXIT=0 in 167 s, so the shared instance’s tree sits near the 30 s teardown budget the test carries and crossed it once under node load (/tmp held 37 GB from other sessions at the time). It did not reproduce, and the fixture was not changed on the strength of a single crossing.

This is the one residue of consolidating four module-scoped instances into one session instance: the shared store is larger when it is removed, so its cleanup has less margin against the default per-test timeout. A node that owns the fixture’s cleanup should give it a background removal or a larger teardown budget; repairing it was not this node’s brief, and the defect did not reproduce when checked.
