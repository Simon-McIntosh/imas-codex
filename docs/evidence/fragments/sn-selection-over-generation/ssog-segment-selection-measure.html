Segment selection measured against the composer on golden A

select_segments was run once per DD source over golden A (golden-name-acceptable.json) and the human sn edit rename targets. Each source's name is compared slot by slot against the target spelling. Two figures come from imas_codex/discovery/base/evaluation.py: the cross-check separation (paired_auc, bootstrap_interval) and cost and latency (cost_latency_summary); the golden A cohort is loaded with load_labelled_fixture. The remaining figures are computed by driver-local helpers, not by evaluation.py: exact-name match and per-slot accuracy and the composer's agreement and per-slot accuracy (_figures, _slot_accuracy, _aggregate), and family agreement (_family_agreement), all in ssog-segment-selection-measure-run.py. Per-slot accuracy is computed by the driver's _slot_accuracy, which delegates the slot comparison to benchmark_roles.exact_match_accuracy (imas_codex/standard_names/benchmark_roles.py) rather than re-implementing it, so the per-slot figures use the project's own match.

Cohort and exclusions

The cohort is every golden A row (291 positives, 446 negatives) plus 336 distinct human-edit rename targets: 1073 rows. A row's DD source is resolved through the name's PRODUCED_NAME provenance, one bounded indexed read per batch of 200. 618 rows carry no DD source and are left out, and 21 further rows' target spelling does not parse canonically, leaving 434 measured rows (167 golden positives, 93 golden negatives, 174 edit targets). The 618 is the dominant exclusion and is a property of the fixture, not of Jev: a name that never reached a DD source has nothing for a source-driven selection to read.

The composer's figures use its recorded spelling, not a fresh compose (no persist-nothing compose call path was used). For golden A's positives the recorded spelling is the target spellings — the fixture's rows are composer output — so the composer's exact-name match there is 1.0 by construction and is circular; it is therefore not shown as a fair comparator, and only Jev's positives column is reported above. For the edit targets the composer's recorded spelling is the pre-rename from_name: the human renamed it because the spelling was wrong, so its exact-name match is 0.0 by construction too.

The composer column runs over all 174 edit targets — the same sources Jev selects over. A recorded spelling that does not parse canonically supplies no segment, so it matches no slot and stays in the denominator; 126 of the 174 do not parse and are counted as per-slot mismatches, not dropped. The table's composer row is that full-set figure. Restricted to the 48 targets whose pre-rename spelling does parse, the composer's per-slot accuracy is much higher — base 0.708, subject 0.813, component 0.958, operator 0.958, locus 0.688, process 0.896 (slot_accuracy_parsable in the results JSON) — and that is the like-for-like figure for a reader asking how near the composer sat when its output was a parseable name at all. The full-set figures are lower precisely because three quarters of the composer's pre-rename spellings were not parseable canonical names, which is why a human renamed them.

Family agreement

Family agreement groups records into species families (the target's slot tokens with the subject removed) and asks whether every member of a multi-member family receives a uniform (physical_base, operator, locus) triple. The record reports two cohorts, because the driver's family filter (every record with label == 1) is wider than the plan's phrase "the positives", and the two give different family counts:

  • Golden positives only — 167 records in 135 families. 15 families carry more than one member and 7 of those are uniform, an agreement rate of 0.467 (family_agreement.golden_positives in the results JSON). This is the cohort the plan's generator rule reads, since the rule is stated over "the positives".
  • Every label-positive record — 341 records in 182 families (the 167 golden positives plus the 174 human-edit targets). 101 families carry more than one member and 81 of those are uniform, an agreement rate of 0.802 (family_agreement.label_1_all). This is the natural reading of "label == 1" and is the wider cohort.

The seventeen-member boundary-density family is the exception the plan names: among the measured rows only 8 of its 17 members carry a DD source (the other 9 are among the 618 exclusions), and those 8 do not agree — they split across four distinct (number_density, operator, locus) appearance combinations (flux_surface_averaged/separatrix, flux_surface_averaged/none, none/isotope, none/none), so Jev does not render the boundary densities uniform. The 0.467 and 0.802 figures above both count that disagreement the same way; only the cohort they are computed over differs.

Cross-check separation

The cross-check signal is the six-slot agreement between Jev's selection and the composer's recorded spelling. Over the 260 golden rows that resolve to a DD source (167 positives, 93 negatives), flagging composer-against-Jev disagreement separates the negatives from the positives with AUC 0.480, bootstrap 95% interval [0.408, 0.552] (paired_auc against the mirror agreement arm gives the same figure, difference −0.0397; bootstrap_interval resamples by copy_group, 1000 draws, seed 123). A value at 0.5 is no separation at all.

Cost

The call layer booked USD 0.261844 across the 434 measured names (cost_latency_summary over the 14 batches: mean USD 0.018703 and median 20.3 s per batch), i.e. USD 0.000603 per name. The node declared the rule to stop if the 10-item pilot's extrapolation exceeded USD 2.00: the pilot measured USD 0.002943 for the 5 of its 10 rows that had a DD source (USD 0.000589 per name), extrapolating to about USD 0.63 for the full cohort, under the ceiling, so the run proceeded. Total spend on this node's measurement, including the pilot and two superseded runs whose cost accounting was discarded, is about USD 0.59, under the USD 2.00 ceiling.

Recommendation

The plan fixes the rule before the run, and it is applied mechanically here:

Cross-check if flagging composer-against-Jev disagreement separates golden A's negatives from its positives with AUC at or above 0.70, and the interval's lower bound is above 0.60. Generator if Jev's exact-name match on the positives is within 0.02 of the composer's, and its family agreement is higher. Both if both conditions hold; neither if neither does.

neither. The cross-check condition fails: AUC 0.480 is below 0.70 and the interval's lower bound 0.408 is below 0.60 — composer-against-Jev disagreement does not separate the negatives from the positives. The generator condition fails: Jev's exact-name match on the positives is 0.0299 against the composer's, which is 1.0 by construction for those rows (the fixture's positives are composer output), and 0.0299 is not within 0.02 of it. Family agreement is read on the golden-positives cohort the rule names (0.467 on 167 records in 135 families). It is not in question either way because the generator's exact-name conjunct already fails, so the verdict does not turn on which cohort the family figure is taken over. Neither deployment is supported on this evidence.

The controlling caveat is the exclusion rate: 618 of 1073 rows have no DD source, and 21 more do not parse, so the measured 434 are the subset whose names reached a source at all. The figures describe that subset, not the whole fixture.
