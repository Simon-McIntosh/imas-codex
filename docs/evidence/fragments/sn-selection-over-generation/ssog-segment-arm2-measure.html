The second segment-selection arm measured against the first arm and the composer on golden A

The second arm is what select_segments now implements: a mandatory base offered over an embedding shortlist of the source's most similar base tokens, every option carrying its meaning, and each later slot offered only tokens that compose with those already chosen. This node runs the first arm's driver on it.

Cohort and exclusions

The cohort is every golden A row plus the distinct human sn edit rename targets: 1075 rows. A row's DD source is resolved through the name's PRODUCED_NAME provenance, one bounded indexed read per batch of 200. 620 rows carry no DD source and are left out, and 21 further rows' target spelling does not parse canonically, leaving 434 measured rows (166 golden positives, 93 golden negatives, 175 edit targets).

The exclusions differ slightly from the first arm's. The graph has grown since the first arm ran: its cohort was 1073 rows (618 with no DD source) against 1075 (620) here, so this run measures 166 golden positives against the first arm's 167 and 175 edit targets against its 174. The DD-source resolution and the parse rule are the same; the fixture and the rename log are not frozen.

One measurement repair, disclosed. The driver's resume path skips rows whose target was already measured, so after the run was interrupted and resumed it had recorded only 386 of the 434 rows — the 48 dropped rows were duplicate-target rows whose twin had already been measured (39 edit rows, 9 golden). Since a row's selection is a function of its target's DD source, which is a function of the target name alone, those rows' measured fields are their twins' exactly; the full 434-row cohort was restored by rebuilding each row from its target's representative before the figures below were computed. The measurement cost is unchanged (no further paid call). The defect is in the copied resume logic, not in the arm.

Where the second arm gains and loses, by slot

Against the first arm on the edit targets:

  • physical_base — a large loss. 0.137 against 0.506, a fall of 0.369, and the run's defining result. The base is the one slot the second arm restricts to an embedding shortlist, and the shortlist offered the target's own base in only 117 of 434 rows (retrieval_hit_rate 0.270) — so for 317 rows the correct base was never on offer and could not be chosen. The loss is a retrieval failure, not a judgement one, and the item's base_in_shortlist flag is what separates them.
  • component — a gain. 0.737 against 0.563 (+0.174); on the positives 0.663 against 0.563.
  • operator — a gain. 0.674 against 0.575 (+0.100); on the positives 0.813 against 0.665 (+0.149), the second arm's strongest slot.
  • locus — a small loss. 0.463 against 0.477 (−0.014); on the positives it gains slightly (0.530 against 0.503).
  • subject — flat. 0.531 against 0.535; positives 0.602 against 0.605.
  • process — flat and the strongest slot. 0.811 against 0.805; positives 0.807 against 0.784.

The pattern is coherent with the design: the slots the second arm constrains (component, operator, via grammar-valid composing options) improve, and the one slot it narrows (the base, via the shortlist) degrades so far that it costs more than those gains recover.

Family agreement

Family agreement groups records into species families (the target's slot tokens with the subject removed) and asks whether every member of a multi-member family receives a uniform (physical_base, operator, locus) triple.

  • Golden positives only — 166 records in 134 families. 15 families carry more than one member and 10 of those are uniform, an agreement rate of 0.667, against the first arm's 0.467. This is the cohort the plan's generator rule reads.
  • Every label-positive record — 341 records in 183 families. 100 families carry more than one member and 91 are uniform, an agreement rate of 0.910, against the first arm's 0.802.

The seventeen-member boundary-density family is the exception the plan names: among the measured rows only 8 of its 17 members carry a DD source, and those 8 do not agree — the second arm splits them across four combinations (intensity/none/none, li/none/none, number_density/none/separatrix, number_density/none/none). Its base loss is visible here: where the first arm put three of the four on number_density, the second arm puts two on bases (intensity, li) that are neither the target's nor each other's. So the second arm's higher family agreement is real on the whole cohort but does not extend to the family the plan singles out.

Cross-check separation

The cross-check signal is the six-slot agreement between the selection and the composer's recorded spelling. On golden A the composer's recorded spelling is the very name the fixture labels, so this AUC measures only whether the selection disagrees more with the rejected names than with the accepted ones. Over the 259 golden rows that resolve to a DD source (166 positives, 93 negatives), flagging composer-against-selection disagreement separates the negatives from the positives with AUC 0.502, bootstrap 95% interval [0.424, 0.574] (paired_auc; bootstrap_interval resamples by copy_group, 1000 draws, seed 123). A value at 0.5 is no separation at all — the second arm is indistinguishable from chance on this signal, as the first arm was.

Cost

The call layer booked USD 0.211233 across the 434 measured names (13 batches), i.e. USD 0.000487 per name, against the first arm's USD 0.261844 and USD 0.000603 per name. The node declared the rule to stop if the 10-item pilot's extrapolation exceeded USD 1.00: the pilot measured USD 0.002786 for the 5 of its 10 rows that had a DD source (USD 0.000557 per name), extrapolating to about USD 0.24 for the full cohort, under the ceiling, so the run proceeded. Total spend on this node's measurement is USD 0.211233 of a measured run (plus USD 0.002786 of pilot), under the USD 1.00 ceiling.

Recommendation

The plan fixes the rule before the run, and it is applied mechanically here:

Cross-check if flagging composer-against-Jev disagreement separates golden A's negatives from its positives with AUC at or above 0.70, and the interval's lower bound is above 0.60. Generator if Jev's exact-name match on the positives is within 0.02 of the composer's, and its family agreement is higher. Both if both conditions hold; neither if neither does.

neither. The cross-check condition fails: AUC 0.502 is below 0.70 and the interval's lower bound 0.424 is below 0.60 — the second arm's disagreement with the composer does not separate the negatives from the positives. The generator condition fails: the second arm's exact-name match on the positives is 0.0241 against the composer's, which is 1.0 by construction for those rows (the fixture's positives are composer output), and 0.0241 is not within 0.02 of it. Family agreement is read on the golden-positives cohort the rule names (0.667 on 166 records in 134 families), higher than the first arm's — but the generator's exact-name conjunct already fails, so the verdict does not turn on it. Neither deployment is supported on the second arm's evidence either.

Beside the first arm, the second arm trades a base for two slots. It selects component and operator better and physical_base far worse, and its exact-name match on the positives falls rather than rises (0.0241 against 0.0299). The base loss is traced to the shortlist: it offered the target's own base in only 27% of rows. If the shortlist is widened or its ranking improved, the arm's base figure is the number to watch — the mechanism, not the model, is what the loss sits on.

The controlling caveat is the same as the first arm's: 620 of 1075 rows have no DD source and 21 more do not parse, so the measured 434 are the subset whose names reached a source at all. The figures describe that subset, not the whole fixture.
