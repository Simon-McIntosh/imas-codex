The contradiction veto on electrostatic_potential is wrong: it read a family base name's generality as a contradiction

A Jev contradiction veto held electrostatic_potential at reviewed despite a judged documentation score of 0.9521, above the 0.85 acceptance floor, and that hold was the one thing stopping the locus family electrostatic_potential converging. The ruling is wrong: the three evidence sentences the judge answered contradicts to do not contradict any of the name's seven DD source bindings. Each restates what every bound source defines, says the general quantity is not restricted to any one of the regions its bindings span, or states that averaging is not implicit in the unqualified quantity — all of them non-requirement sentences about a base name that is legitimately broader than any single binding. The two open holds were dismissed through the hold channel's own resolution path with the ruling as the reason, which promoted the document to accepted; the family was then re-applied under a USD 2.00 cap.

Ruling: the veto is wrong. A general base name may legitimately be broader than any single DD binding, and this one spans bindings whose own texts differ in exactly the two respects the judge read as contradictions: which region the value is evaluated in, and whether it is averaged. The judge compared the base name's stored documentation against the most restrictive single binding, plasma_profiles/profiles_1d/phi_potential, and answered contradicts where the sentences agree with that binding wherever it applies and with the other six everywhere else. Nothing in the documentation asserts a claim any of the seven sources denies.

The shipped settlement should have answered “says nothing”

This is not a judgement call the cascade was intended to route to the judge at all. Sentences 2 and 3 state non-requirements: that the quantity imposes no locus, and that no averaging is implied. The veto's shipped settlement exists for exactly that form — a claim stating a non-requirement settles code-side as says nothing with settled_by: "non_requirement_sentence", under two conditions: the documented name carries no locus or aggregation qualifier of its own, and the sentence states a non-requirement rather than asserting the quantity's own combination. Both conditions hold here — name_fixes_locus_or_aggregation("electrostatic_potential") is False.

Measured against this head's own predicate (claim_settlement in imas_codex/standard_names/review/jev_review.py), the settlement does not fire on any of the three sentences, nor on any of the thirteen claims the stored documentation splits into: all thirteen return None, leaving every one for the judge. The cause is the predicate's grammar, not its intent. It recognises a negated requirement only through the verb list prescrib…|impos…|requir…|mandat…|demand…|specif… or a “no mandatory …” form; these sentences negate restricted and implicit, neither of which is in that list, so two true non-requirement sentences pass to the judge and one of them is answered contradicts.

Positive control that the instrument sees what is present. Two claims asserting the quantity's own combination — “only the flux-surface average in the core plasma and never a local value” and “only the temperature” — also return None from claim_settlement, which is correct: they are the judge's to read, and the settlement declining them is what shows the None above is a reading of the sentences rather than a predicate that returns nothing at all. The settlement is not silent by accident; it is silent on the wrong sentences.

Both are fans of one defect — a veto firing where no source is denied — and both are precision misses for the same calibration, but the mechanism differs, so S0010's fix does not address it. The remedy this case points to is the settlement's grammar, not the probability channel's cutoff.
