The convergence gate halted the locus family on state the campaign did not write

The electrostatic_potential re-measure (67a45b57-d1ad-48f7-b904-f273e7fac30f and c1560b3c-54c7-4a28-b40b-075d97b17acd) spent USD 0.2603 over 17 LLMCost calls and then refused to converge on two reasons, both of which are defects in the gate rather than in the family. The name axis was never written — it is a docs-only campaign — so the two names the drift clause reported carry the stage they already had. And the acceptance clause counted only documents the pipeline had stamped accepted, so a document its review judged at 0.9521 against a 0.85 floor, held one round for a contradiction refinement, was counted as a failure. The gate now compares the state a batch inherited against the state it left, and counts a held review at or above the acceptance score as an acceptance. No threshold moved.

Cause 1: the drift clause read an absolute stage

measure_batch (imas_codex/standard_names/campaign.py) walked the batch's member ids and appended a name to BatchOutcome.name_drift whenever the refreshed row was missing, or whenever its name_stage was anything other than accepted. That is a test of absolute post-drain state. A docs-only campaign never writes the name axis (the invariant on the run lists the family's five names with their stage fields untouched), so a member that was already outside accepted when the family was planned could only ever be reported as drift. Both names the halt named — electrostatic_potential_at_outboard_midplane and electrostatic_potential_at_wall — were at reviewed before the drain and at reviewed after it. Neither is in touched_names: the campaign wrote no documentation for either, because neither could be claimed.

The clause now asks whether the name's stage moved. The runner reads the batch's member rows once before anything in the batch touches them and passes that read to measure_batch as prior_rows; a name is drift when its post-drain stage differs from the inherited one and the new stage is not accepted. A name with no inherited row, or absent from the post-drain read, is still drift, because the gate cannot otherwise distinguish an unreadable name from an untouched one. A name the batch itself moves out of accepted still halts.

Planning also stops offering such members to a docs-only batch. plan_family_applies now reads the members' name stages and splits them: only accepted members enter the batch list, and the rest are carried in the plan under skipped with their stage, so the family's outcome names them rather than the gate halting on them. A stage the read cannot answer for is treated as eligible — an absent answer is not evidence that the member is excluded, and dropping it silently would be the same defect in the other direction.

Cause 2: the acceptance clause ignored the score the review wrote

The halt's (1/2 touched) is the member batch: electrostatic_potential and floating_electrostatic_potential were touched, one was counted accepted. The family totals of 2 of 3 include the anchor batch, which carried the third name at 1 of 1. So the member that counted as touched-and-not-accepted is electrostatic_potential, the family's own base, and the reason is not a miscount but a definition. measure_batch incremented accepted only for docs_stage == "accepted". That name's document came back from review at 0.9521, well above the 0.85 acceptance floor, and the pipeline held it at reviewed rather than promoting it because a contradiction veto sent it round a refinement. The score and the stage disagreed, and the clause read the stage.

It now reads the score too. A document at reviewed whose recorded reviewer_score_docs is at or above the acceptance score counts as accepted, and the member ids are listed on BatchOutcome.score_accepted so a hold stays visible in the outcome rather than being absorbed silently. A reviewed document below the floor is still an acceptance failure, and any other stage is still a failure. The floor is DEFAULT_MIN_SCORE, the same constant the pipeline applies.
