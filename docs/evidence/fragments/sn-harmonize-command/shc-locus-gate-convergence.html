The locus gate flags a definition, not every mention of the locus

Diagnoses why the boundary anchor's documentation never cleared the locus_definition gate across repeated apply attempts, and narrows the gate to the case it was written for. Write paths: imas_codex/standard_names/docs_gates.py, tests/standard_names/test_docs_locus_definition.py.

Confirmed at imas_codex/standard_names/docs_gates.py: _locus_definition_findings tested the locus phrase (\b{phrase}\b) and a copula (\b(is|are)\b), independently over the same sentence. Any locus mention sharing a sentence with an is or are was therefore a candidate second definition. A house-style opening — "The flux-surface-averaged xenon density at the plasma boundary is the poloidal flux-surface average of the local xenon ion number density …" — matched both patterns and does not carry the registry definition, so the gate fired on it. Nothing in the check distinguished what the locus is from what a quantity is, evaluated there.

The diagnosis is that the target moved rather than that the sentence was stubborn. Each of the anchor's four stored DocsRevision texts fired on a different sentence:

Change: the gate now requires the locus phrase to be the subject the copula predicates on, after one determiner is stripped. The pattern admits up to two words between the phrase and the copula, so "The plasma-boundary surface is …" is still a definition, and the word governing the phrase is inspected: a phrase preceded by a preposition ("the density at the plasma boundary is …") names where a quantity is evaluated and is no longer read as a second definition. Semantics kept: a sentence stating the locus in other words is still flagged, the registry's own sentence is still accepted, and the forbidden variant still arrives through forbidden_locus_gloss.
