The owners, named, and where each one stops

  1. The PRODUCED_NAME move has an owner and it is not the obstacle. retarget_standard_name_sources (imas_codex/standard_names/provenance_lifecycle.py:505) moves PRODUCED_NAME, its scalar mirror, the upstream HAS_STANDARD_NAME projection and both names' source_paths in one compare-and-set transaction, and verifies the count. It requires the target to be live: when the only live holder is the old name and the new name is retired it raises SourceRetiredSuccessorConflictError. That is why the revival is a precondition rather than a parallel step.

  2. The revive owner is the fold-revival inside supersede_into (imas_codex/standard_names/edit.py:2886, the writer behind imas-codex sn supersede; target-tombstone revival at edit.py:2931-2945, stage chosen by _fold_revival_stage at edit.py:2212-2231). Folding a live name into a superseded tombstone re-occupies the tombstone instead of leaving carried sources with no fold. Every donor the family offers was exercised in preview (dry_run=True, no write):

    donor (old)target (into)verdict
    distance_at_outboard_midplane radial_separatrix_distance_at_outboard_midplane ok=false — "superseded identity has missing or ambiguous fold ledger"
    radial_gap radial_separatrix_distance_at_outboard_midplane ok=false — "name 'radial_gap' has another successor lineage; fold is ambiguous"

    So there is no admissible donor in this family: one retired member lacks a clean fold ledger, the other carries a competing successor lineage. No live name outside the family may be sacrificed to revive a tombstone, because folding destroys the donor.

  3. And even with an admissible donor, the owner's revival stage is terminal for this input. _fold_revival_stage (edit.py:2212-2231) returns target_properties["superseded_from_stage"] or "drafted", capped only by _FOLD_REVIVAL_STAGE_CAP = {"accepted": "reviewed"} (edit.py:2144). This tombstone records superseded_from_stage = "superseded", which is not a live stage and is not in the cap, so the revival would write name_stage = "superseded" while supersede_into resets the catalog status to draft (its own comment at edit.py:2936-2945 exists precisely to keep those two axes consistent). The name would end live on neither axis. This is the defect, and it is in the owner rather than in the data path.

What would unblock it, and why this node did not do it

The codebase's own refusals name the alternative route twice — the rename refusal at edit.py:3209 ("return it to draft with a signed transition in imas_codex/standard_names/signed_manifest.py") and the rescore refusal at graph_ops.py:24587. The signed repair envelope in imas_codex/standard_names/signed_manifest.py carries lifecycle authority (signed-lifecycle-and-claim) and admits a set_properties mutation, which is enough to move the tombstone to a live stage and status in one signed, receipt-bearing transaction. Two routes are therefore open, and both are the coordinator's to choose because both change a lifecycle rule rather than apply one:

  • Fix the owner: the fallback in _fold_revival_stage (edit.py:2230) should treat a recorded prior stage that is itself terminal as absent and revive to drafted, exactly as it already does when the field was never set. A one-line change, and it repairs every identity tombstoned twice, not only this one.
  • Or authorise the signed transition: a set_properties repair row returning this tombstone to name_stage='drafted', status='draft', superseded_from_stage=null, after which the move follows through retarget_standard_name_sources unchanged.

Neither was taken: the brief reserves a change to an owner — and any choice between two lifecycle semantics — to the coordinator, and instructs the worker to stop and report the owner and the reason instead. Hand Cypher is excluded by the same brief, and no writer is live to argue a case for either semantics on merit.
