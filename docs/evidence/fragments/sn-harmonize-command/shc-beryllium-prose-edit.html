Number density of beryllium

The flux-surface average is defined by

$$
\langle n_{\mathrm{Be}}\rangle = \oint n_{\mathrm{Be}}\,dS/|\nabla\psi| \, / \, \oint dS/|\nabla\psi|
$$

where $\langle n_{\mathrm{Be}}\rangle$ is the averaged beryllium-ion number density, $n_{\mathrm{Be}}$ is the local beryllium-ion number density summed over ion charge states, $S$ is the plasma-boundary flux surface, $dS$ is its surface element, and $\psi$ is the poloidal magnetic flux whose gradient magnitude provides the weighting.

This quantity is a charge-state-summed surface average, not a local point value, a volume average, or a density that includes neutral beryllium. It is obtained from the local [beryllium ion density at the plasma boundary](name:beryllium_density_at_plasma_boundary) by replacing the pointwise value with the inverse-poloidal-flux-gradient-weighted surface mean. It differs from the [volume-averaged beryllium density](name:volume_averaged_beryllium_density), which averages over the confined plasma volume.

The last closed magnetic flux surface: the outermost flux surface on which field lines close on themselves, bounding the confined plasma. Where $\psi = \psi_{\mathrm{LCFS}}$, $\psi$ is poloidal magnetic flux; $\psi_{\mathrm{LCFS}}$ is its value on the last closed flux surface.
