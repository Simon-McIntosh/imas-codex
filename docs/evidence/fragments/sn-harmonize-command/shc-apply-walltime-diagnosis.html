Where an apply's wall time goes, and why a quarantined member blocks the family stamp

Two approved spending runs are blocked, and neither block is spend. The boundary-averaged density apply (r-20261015T194702454954-shc-boundary-final-apply) is wall-time bound: 1,200 s of an unforced apply produced one member — the anchor, under review and not accepted. The three-family measure (r-20261015T194835422080-shc-three-family-measure) is blocked because a terminal family member cannot pass revalidation, so the density family cannot be stamped. This fragment gives the mechanism and the smallest change for each. Every figure cites a log line or a bounded graph read.

1. Wall time: a fixed per-invocation startup, paid once per batch, plus a per-member review cycle

sn harmonize --apply enters apply_families (imas_codex/standard_names/harmonize.py:1512). That function builds one CampaignRunner per family and calls runner.run(...) (harmonize.py:1606). CampaignRunner.run (imas_codex/standard_names/campaign.py:929) loops the batches of the anchor-gated plan (campaign.py:989) and, per batch, calls drain_fn(run_id, cap) (campaign.py:1016). The drain is _docs_drain_factory._drain (imas_codex/cli/sn.py:823), which calls _run_sn_cmd (sn.py:846), which starts the six-pool orchestrator run_sn_pools (imas_codex/standard_names/loop.py:1313) from the top. There is no run-scoped cache: every batch pays the full CLI-and-pool startup again, because the drain is a fresh process-level call to run_sn_pools, whose reconcile-once-at-startup block (loop.py:1688 onward) and pool seeding run each time.

The run's own captures split the time into three parts:

  • Fixed startup, ~8 minutes per drain call. Attempt 1 (563 s total) spent "~8 min of that 563 s on fixed startup (graph resolve, family lint, derive_edges over the catalog, pool start) before the scoped run began at 22:03:46 local; only ~90 s of LLM work followed" (capture-02b-apply-attempts.txt). The graph resolve is visible in the shared CLI log as the repeated imas_codex.remote.tunnel: Local squeue found service node / imas_codex.graph.profiles: SLURM: Neo4j resolved to bolt://… pairs that precede each reset line (~/.local/share/imas-codex/logs/sn_sn-harmonize.log:939–992). The derive_edges pass is imas_codex.standard_names.derivation.derive_edges (derivation.py:92), invoked from the family assembly / lint path.
  • Per-member review cycle, ~20 minutes for the anchor. Attempt 2 ran 1,200 s and its last heartbeat read in_flight=1 started=13 completed=12 failed=0 spend=$1.0069; the anchor's docs cycled reviewed (score=0.849) at chain 0/3 then 1/3 and the review held from acceptance on a locus_definition finding, with names_composed=0 (capture-02b-apply-attempts.txt). The 13 started / 12 completed calls in 1,200 s are the generate→review→refine chain re-running because the review will not accept; names_composed=0 means the member never left the cycle.
  • Two resets per attempt, one per batch. The shared log shows four reset_standard_name_docs lines, all 1/1, two per apply attempt (sn_sn-harmonize.log:960, 965, 973, 978, at 21:56:51, 21:58:45, 22:10:33 and 22:18:43 local). Each is mark_members_for_regen (harmonize.py:677) → reset_standard_name_docs (harmonize.py:695) for one batch. In attempt 2 the two resets are 8 min apart (22:10:33 → 22:18:43), consistent with the anchor batch's drain taking that long before the next batch was marked.

Classification of the cost. The startup is fixed per drain call, which in the anchor-gated plan is per batch — a family is at least two batches (the anchor, then the members; apply_families docstring, harmonize.py:1528–1536), so a family pays the fixed cost twice. It is not fixed per member and not once per campaign pass. The residual cost is per member: the generate→review→refine cycle re-runs whenever review does not accept, and this re-run — not the fixed startup — dominates the per-member time.

17-member estimate. Fixed: ~8 min × 2 batches ≈ 16 min. Per member: the measured rate is ~20 min per member (1,200 s → one member, not accepted; capture-02b-apply-attempts.txt). For 17 members that is ≈ 340 min. Total ≈ 6 hours for the unforced 17-member boundary apply. This is consistent with the two prior full applies on this family, which spent USD 2.71 and USD 6.26 — "consistent with hours of wall time" (capture-02b-apply-attempts.txt). The bound is wall time, not spend: the dry run estimates USD 7.04, inside the USD 8.50 ceiling.

Smallest change (operational + scope decision). Operational: run the apply on a long-lived runner that is not bounded by the harness per-command limit (600 s foreground + 600 s background), with --cost-limit 8.5 and no --force — both failures were kills, not product faults (retry_failures in r-20261015T194702454954-shc-boundary-final-apply/manifest.md: EXIT=124 at 563 s, then the harness cap at 1,200 s). Product follow-on for the lead to size: the per-batch re-entry into run_sn_pools (campaign.py:1016 → sn.py:823) makes a family pay startup twice; a run-scoped reuse would remove one ~8-minute startup per family. That is a product change with a test that drives apply_families with a counting drain_fn and asserts it is entered once per family rather than once per batch.

2. A quarantined, exhausted member blocks the family stamp

Pattern-family membership is enumerated by fetch_pattern_family → _PATTERN_FAMILY_MEMBERS_QUERY (imas_codex/standard_names/families.py:605 and :547). That query keeps every name whose name_stage is not $superseded (families.py:549, 554), and _LIVE_STAGE_EXCLUSION = "superseded" (families.py:407). The stamp query inside stamp_harmonized uses the same single exclusion, coalesce(c.name_stage, '') <> 'superseded' (harmonize.py:729), and defers any family in which a member's docs_stage is not accepted (harmonize.py:744–748). An exhausted name is not superseded, so it is enumerated as a live family member and its non-acceptance defers the whole family.

The three-family measure died one step earlier, inside the campaign loop. After the drain, CampaignRunner.run calls revalidate_fn(gc, reintroduced_ids, clean_ids) (campaign.py:1056) = default_revalidate (campaign.py:719). When any id in clean_ids is still validation_status='quarantined', it raises rather than confirming it (campaign.py:759–764). The density apply (SLURM job 1286605) died on exactly this: 

ValueError: cannot confirm quarantined standard-name identities as valid:
  volume_averaged_electron_number_density_over_scrape_off_layer
EXIT=1 WALL_SECONDS=1385

Source: density-apply.slurm.log (tail), which also shows names_reviewed=5 — five members reviewed, so the sixth (the exhausted one) could not be lifted from quarantine and the revalidation refused the batch. Because the raise aborts the run, the family is never stamped; and even if it did not raise, the stamp would defer on the same member at harmonize.py:744.

Should an exhausted name be a family member at all? The domain audit answers no. LIVE_NAME_STAGES = {"accepted", "reviewed", "drafted", "pending"} (imas_codex/standard_names/domain_audit.py:61) — its comment states plainly that "a superseded or exhausted name is not live" (domain_audit.py:56–58). The family enumeration and the stamp query exclude only superseded, so they disagree with the audit's own live-name definition: an exhausted member is treated as live by the harmonizer and dead by the domain audit. The inconsistency is the mechanism — the family code applies a weaker liveness test than the rest of the package.

Smallest change (scope decision for the lead, with a product shape). A scope decision is required because the two defensible repairs are different in kind: (a) align the family code with the domain audit — exclude exhausted (and superseded) from _PATTERN_FAMILY_MEMBERS_QUERY (families.py:549, 554, 407) and from the stamp query (harmonize.py:729), using LIVE_NAME_STAGES as the shared predicate. Then the density family becomes six members and the locus family three, and both can stamp; or (b) repair the three exhausted identities outside the harmonize path (they are terminal at the review-attempt cap, per imas_codex/settings.py:1419) and re-run. Option (a) is the product change the evidence supports: it is the smallest change, it makes the harmonizer agree with the audit's own live-name definition, and it removes the block without touching name identities. Its test would assert that a family containing one exhausted member enumerates and stamps on its remaining live members, and that fetch_pattern_family omits the exhausted id — a fixture graph with one exhausted member beside one accepted, checked so the family still forms. Option (b) is the lead's call if the exhausted names should be adjudicated rather than retired from the family.

Summary of recommendations. Wall time: operational — run the apply on a long-lived runner (--cost-limit 8.5, no --force); product follow-on — remove the per-batch re-entry into run_sn_pools so a family pays startup once. Quarantine block: scope decision for the lead — align family membership and the stamp to LIVE_NAME_STAGES so an exhausted name is not a live family member; product change in families.py + harmonize.py with a fixture-graph test.
