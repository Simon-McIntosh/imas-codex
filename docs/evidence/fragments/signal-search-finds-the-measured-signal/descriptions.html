JT-60SA signal descriptions after a boundary-respecting glossary

The glossary that steers signal enrichment matched its terms as case-folded substrings of the catalogue text, so a two- or three-letter abbreviation reached every signal whose path happened to spell it: DR reached 522 signals and EFC reached 203, including ones named cryohrs_cdpt171 and refcurEF1. This node replaces the substring test with one that holds a term to whole words and camel-case boundaries, adds the JT-60SA abbreviations the census showed were missing or wrong (SNU, EFC, BPS, QPC, DR, HTS, FPPCC), and re-enriches the signals the census flagged under that glossary.

Description census, before and after re-enrichment

The census is restricted to the signals each abbreviation actually belongs to: the family is the set of eligible signals whose id or name reaches the term under the boundary matcher — 182 for EFC/EFCC, 56 for SNU. Restricting it matters: a case-folded efc selector also pulls in refcuref1bst ("EF1 booster power supply") and the CS1–CS4 base supplies, which are equilibrium field and central solenoid supplies, not error field correction at all.

Their descriptions name the supplies they are.

The matcher and its tests

glossary_term_reaches(term, text) accepts a match that begins a word — at the start, after a non-alphanumeric, or at a lower-case-to-upper-case turn — and does not run into a following lower-case run. The boundary test passes against it and fails against the substring matcher: with the substring test restored, two tests fail (test_the_boundary_matcher_holds_a_term_to_whole_words, test_a_coil_abbreviation_reaches_its_own_signal_not_a_lookalike) and the declared string holds — the substring test admits EFC reaching refcurEF1.
