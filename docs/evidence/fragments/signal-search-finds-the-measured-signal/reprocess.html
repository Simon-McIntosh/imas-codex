Reprocessing a stratified JT-60SA signal sample

202 JT-60SA signals were drawn at random within each source category — LCDB, edas, UDDB, G-EQDSK and EQDB — so the sample carries every scanner the facility has. All 202 are grouped catalogue members. The sample was reset to discovered once, re-enriched through the current pipeline, and then checked signal by signal.

The reset is the one step that could have silently done nothing, so it carries a negative control. Before the fix whose effect this node measures, 1441 JT-60SA signals were enriched before 2026-10-11T21:16:46Z. A census taken immediately after the reset found 202 of the sample's signals at discovered and 0 with a description — the complement of the control, so a census that had found them still described would have meant the reset never ran.

Every one of the 202 sample signals was checked afterwards. All are at enriched, all 202 carry a description, none has an embedded_text_hash that disagrees with a hash recomputed from its own description (0 mismatches), none was left enriched from before the reprocess start (0), and none is without a signal_role (0).

What the facility would cost

Projected over all 15,111 JT-60SA signals: Jev spend $2.32 and wall time 1.7 hours on the run's own record basis (0.00015371 per signal, 146.3/min). On the sample-signal basis ($0.00084158 per signal, 26.7/min) the same projection reads $12.72 and 9.4 hours. The two bases differ because the run's record counts the members its individualisation reached; they are the same measurement over different denominators.

Total spend for this node was $0.1709, against a ceiling of USD 2. The seed half cost nothing — its scanner reads the facility's own catalogue and calls no model — and the enrichment's spend is Jev's relevance rerank over the retrieved context.
