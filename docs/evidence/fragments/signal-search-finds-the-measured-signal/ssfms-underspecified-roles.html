An underspecified signal is judged for its role, not left role-less

A signal enrichment marks underspecified carries the same stored description a searcher reads, but the role predicate admitted only enriched and checked, and the enrich worker judged a role only on its enriched branch. So an underspecified signal was reachable by text and invisible to a role= filter, and the sweep that backfills missing roles never saw it. This node admits underspecified in the shared predicate and has the enrich worker judge any signal it marks underspecified as it already did for one it marks enriched.

The sweep and its census still share one predicate, extended by one status. A signal is a candidate when status is enriched, checked or underspecified, its description is non-empty, and signal_role is null. The store test now drives its graph double from the Cypher the production sweep and census issue rather than from a predicate of the test's own, so the two are required to agree by construction: the double selects exactly the rows the statement it receives names, and the test fails when a caller drops the underspecified status.

Against the live graph, 210 JT-60SA signals awaited a role — described, underspecified, and role-less. Every one was judged before any write (210/210 accepted, 0 failed), at Jev response-usage spend of USD 0.005393. The judged roles were then stored, and the census read 210 before and 0 after across a population of 15111 JT-60SA signals — zero exactly when the store left nothing.
