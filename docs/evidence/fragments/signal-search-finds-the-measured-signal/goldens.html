Pooled labels on the golden signal fixture

Every arm in tests/llm/test_signal_search_goldens.py is scored on the population the fixture's predicate admits, and every candidate an arm places in its top ten carries a grade. The predicate is declared as data in the fixture (eligibility.facility jt-60sa, eligibility.enriched_at_cutover 2026-10-11T21:08:54Z) and is applied by eligible_signal_ids; filter_eligible removes every excluded candidate from an arm's order before the cut at K, so a signal enriched before the cutover is dropped rather than scored, and the slot it held is taken by the next eligible candidate. current_tool (the product ranking), additive_rule (the product's additive rule frozen in the harness), vector_only (pool 100) and rrf (100-vector fused with a 50-full-text pool) are scored on this population, beside the seeded random control (CONTROL_SEED 20261012). The predicate admits 14312 of the facility's signals; the fixture labels 1551 of them across 22 questions, 11 held out.

additive_rule reproduces the product's ranking arithmetic itself — vector search and full-text search, 0.7 of the cosine plus 0.3 of the text score plus 0.1 for a signal found by both, the text score alone for a text-only signal, cut at K — rather than calling _rank_signals, so the arm keeps measuring the rule the product ships today after the product ranking is rewritten. test_the_frozen_additive_rule_reproduces_the_product_ranking pins the copy to the head it was taken from: the two arms return the same order on every question, which is what makes the frozen arm a faithful stand-in rather than a rewrite.

The two rerank arms move out of the test suite

The section closes on the four arms above and the control. The two rerank arms (rrf_rerank, rrf_rerank_role) are scored by a measurement that runs outside pytest, over labels pooled against its own candidate pool, and no test here reaches the paid endpoint.

Two structural reasons, neither a labelling gap. First, tests/conftest.py installs an autouse fixture, no_live_decisions_requests, that raises on any HTTP request to openrouter.ai/api/alpha/decisions, which is the endpoint both rerank arms spend on, so a pytest run cannot score them by design. Second, the rerank reorders a 60-candidate pool nondeterministically, so any of those 60 can reach the top ten on a given run; the fixture's labels are the top-10 union over the arms, so a rerank run can surface a family the union has not reached — four consecutive live runs each did, and each was labelled before the next moved the boundary again.

The module keeps what that measurement needs — _rerank_arm, the JEV_ARMS tuple, JEV_ARMS_ENV and the role-aware question constants — and arm_ranking still builds a rerank arm when ctx.spend_jev is set. No test sets it, so the committed suite never reaches the guarded endpoint and spends nothing.
