Smoke read: can Jev tell flux-surface-average forms and boundary glosses apart?

Four boundary flux-surface-averaged density entries were each asked three questions in one request. Two were categorising Choices: the form of the flux-surface-average equation, and how the plasma boundary is glossed. One was a narrow Noul: whether the equation weights the line element by the total field B where the poloidal field B_p belongs. The expected answers come from reading the stored documentation. The carbon entry writes \oint n_C\,dl/B, the argon entry the poloidal line form, and the beryllium and electron entries the surface form. All four call the boundary the separatrix.

The two Choices agree with the reading on all four entries. The Noul separates the defective entry only weakly: 0.53 against 0.03 to 0.04, a gap a threshold could use but that sits near indifference. With four entries this is a smoke read, not a measurement. It supports one design choice and nothing more: ask a categorising Choice over the forms a check must tell apart, rather than a narrow yes-or-no on one defect. The beryllium gloss was confirmed in its text ("evaluated on the separatrix, the outermost closed magnetic flux surface"). The same read found a second defect class. The beryllium entry links "beryllium density at the plasma boundary" to its own identity rather than to the unaveraged quantity.
