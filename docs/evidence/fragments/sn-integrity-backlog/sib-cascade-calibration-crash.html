The calibration rebuild finishes inside its timeout and fails as a test

test_matched_policy_rebuilds_from_frozen_scores_and_receipts called cascade_calibration.build() twice. One pass measures 155.67 s under load, so the test spent roughly five minutes of wall time inside a single id, and under xdist the same call is charged against a 600 s mark while seven sibling workers compete for eight cores. The kill that followed is the worker crashed xdist reports: pytest-timeout's thread method terminates the process, so no assertion is ever printed. The test now runs one rebuild and compares it against the committed artifact, which is the stronger repeatability statement — every cutoff, route count and interval must match — and costs one pass instead of two.

Where the time goes

A clean single build() at this revision took 155.67 s with five sibling pytest processes running, and ended ARTIFACT_EQUAL: True. The two costs inside it, each caught by a faulthandler dump with its frame:

  • Grammar registry re-read. The 90 s dump sits at imas_codex/standard_names/review/cascade_calibration.py:275 in _docs_axis, through jev_review.build_docs_audit_report (jev_review.py:940) into audits.run_house_style_checks (audits.py:703) and vocab_loaders.load_conventions (vocab_loaders.py:904), which parses the vocabulary YAML. The pass already wraps that loop in _cached_conventions, and the cache was measured effective: the first run_house_style_checks costs 9.638 s and the next two 0.081 s and 0.085 s. So the registry is one warm-up, not the per-item cost the comment above it claims.
  • The resampling pass. The 120 s dump sits at evaluation.py:214 inside bootstrap_interval, called from cascade_calibration.py:413 in _measure and :449 in _matched_report. RESAMPLES is 10000, and _matched_report runs 25 bootstraps per axis — 18 in three _measure calls, six paired differences and the veto precision — for two axes. Each draw rebuilds its index set with a Python list comprehension over every copy group (evaluation.py:210). That is the pass's dominant cost and it is deterministic work the artifact already records.

The SIGSEGV is not a single event

The census recorded one EXIT=139 and could not attribute it. It is not single. Two of five clean solo runs died with EXIT=139, at 75 s and 78 s, against three that passed at 167 s, 168 s and 160 s. All five ran the same command, alone, with no profiler, under OMP_NUM_THREADS=1. The crash is a live defect at this revision, not an artefact of the earlier session.

The fix

The test's second cc.build() is removed. The remaining assertion, first == json.loads(cc.THRESHOLDS_PATH.read_text()), is a stronger repeatability check than the one it replaces: the committed artifact carries every interval the rebuild computes, so a change to any frozen column, to any threshold, or to the resampling itself fails it.
