Docs search ordered its score ties arbitrarily

_search_docs in imas_codex/llm/search_tools.py ranked wiki chunks with a plain descending score sort, so the many chunks that share a hybrid score came back in set-iteration order, and the unbounded keyword term let keyword-only chunks enter at their raw BM25 score (9 to 16, far above any cosine) and take the whole window. Both effects push the facility's nearest embeddings out of the returned results. The change makes the tie order deterministic by raw cosine and reserves the 20 ranks below the top 10 for the vector leaders.

The defect reproduces

  • Q14 — the answering chunk jt-60sa:use_compiler-en.html:chunk_0 (raw cosine 0.856) is not in the tool's returned k=30 window, because keyword-only chunks outscore the whole clamp tie block.
  • Q18 — the answering chunk jt-60sa:use_login-en.html:chunk_0 (raw cosine 0.882) is likewise absent from the returned window.

Measurement

The measurement reproduces the tool's own wiki-chunk order instead of re-deriving it: docs_ties_eval.py calls the same stage functions _search_docs calls and merges them with the tool's own rule (0.7*vec + 0.3*bm25 + 0.1, rounded, with the title boost excluded because _search_docs fixes the order before it applies the display boost and never re-sorts). Fidelity is asserted before any number is reported: the reimplemented order's top 30 overlaps the rerank prototype's stored docs candidate set at q13 29/30, q14 30/30, q15 30/30, q16 28/30, q17 30/30, q18 30/30 — the shortfall is only where a passing document occupies one of the prototype's 30 slots. nDCG uses a fixed ideal set taken from the whole label pool, so changing which chunks are in the window cannot inflate the score. Relevance labels are the rerank prototype's labels.json (keyed by candidate id) plus the answering chunks it does not cover, labelled by the same rule and written to reports/imas-codex/docs-score-ties/new_labels.json. Because the deployed change is in _search_docs, every mean is over the six docs questions q13–q18, not over all 18.

The displayed top 10 is bit-identical to the current order on every docs question, so nDCG@10 is unchanged (0.4076) and Q16 stays at 0.9675: the change is a preservation of the visible ranking, not a ranking gain — a bare cosine tie-break cannot move the answers, because they sit inside a score tie that the keyword-only chunks outrank as a block. The one effect the change delivers is that the two answering chunks enter the k=30 window, at ranks 15 and 13 (per-question nDCG@30 for Q14 rises 0.0 to 0.25 and for Q18 0.0 to 0.381). That effect carries a measured cost: reserving the 20 ranks below the top 10 displaces relevant chunks that previously sat there, so the docs mean nDCG@30 falls from 0.5298 to 0.4784 (q13 0.6457 to 0.3026, q15 0.9058 to 0.5320, q17 0.6596 to 0.4372). The change is selected by the node's stated rule — keep Q16 at or above its current value and lift the most answers into the top 30 — not by an nDCG gain: the deploy variant lifts two answers, the plain and cosine-only variants lift none.
