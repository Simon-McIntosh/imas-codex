Jev rerank lifts JT-60SA code and docs retrieval

An earlier draft of this record ordered the embedding baseline by descending rank, which is not the order the tool returns; this record replaces it and every embedding figure below is the corrected one.

Eighteen natural-language questions about JT-60SA — twelve answerable from ingested code and six from ingested wiki and document content — were run through the production search tools (_search_code / _search_docs in imas_codex/llm/search_tools.py, facility="jt-60sa", k=30) to give each question thirty candidates in the tool's own hybrid order. Every candidate was hand-labelled relevant or not against the question's answer sentence, from its title and chunk text; the labels and the judged text are in labels.json. Each candidate was then scored by one Jev Score (POST https://openrouter.ai/api/alpha/decisions, model typesafe/jev-1.13) on a five-level scale from "unrelated to the query" to "directly answers the query", the state carrying the query and the candidate's path or title with the first 1500 characters of its text. nDCG@10 and mean reciprocal rank are reported for the embedding order and for the order by Jev score, per question and averaged.

Averaged over all eighteen questions, the Jev rerank raises nDCG@10 from 0.246 to 0.508 and mean reciprocal rank from 0.384 to 0.544. Restricted to the 13 questions with at least one relevant candidate retrieved, the rise is larger: nDCG@10 0.340 → 0.704, MRR 0.531 → 0.753. The paired per-question difference in nDCG@10 (Jev minus embedding) is 0.364 (95% bootstrap CI [0.219, 0.517], 13 questions, P(Δ≤0) = 0.000). Per question the rerank is higher on 11 of the 13 answerable questions, lower on 1 (Q10) and equal on 1 (Q03). Five questions retrieved no relevant candidate at all in the top thirty; they are a retrieval miss the rerank cannot repair, and they are held in every figure above rather than dropped.

Negative control: ordering each question's same thirty candidates by a random score instead of the Jev score leaves the metric at the floor. Over 200 fresh random permutations the mean nDCG@10 is 0.207 on all questions (0.286 on the answerable subset) — below the embedding order's 0.246 — while the Jev rerank reaches 0.508. An arbitrary order therefore does not recover the gain, and the embedding order carries a little signal above chance (0.246 vs 0.207); the metric distinguishes the useful order from an arbitrary one. The control row of the averages table and the control column of the per-question table are one fixed-seed random draw (0.241 on all questions, 0.334 answerable); the 200-permutation figures above are the expected value of a random order.

The rerank scored 540 of 540 requested candidates, one Jev Score request each, at a total transport-reported cost of $0.0190. Per-question wall latency is in the table; across all 540 calls the per-call p50 is about 0.45 s and p95 under 1.2 s.
