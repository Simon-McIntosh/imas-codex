The two misses are already candidates; they are ranked out

Three corrections, added at merge after review. The two answers differ in how they are lost. eqgrd.f (Q05) is already in the merged pool at the current vector cut of 30, at rank 85 of 135. shtrd.f (Q12) is not in the pool at cut 30 and enters only at a cut of 50, at merged rank 113. The live rerun matches this harness order exactly on 5 questions (q03, q04, q05, q07, q08), not 6, and q10 differs within its top 10, not only inside score ties. The per-query values in variants.log come from a local-denominator nDCG and do not match the table; metrics.json holds the figures the table reports.

§12's premise is only half right. Merging the tool's stages without its final cap shows both answers are generated by the current code path, then lost to the top-30 cut:

  • Q05 eqgrd.f is retrieved today by the hybrid text stage at merged score 0.797, rank 85 of a 135-candidate pool at cut30 — it is not in the vector stage until a cut of 500 (vector score 0.724, merged rank 71). Widening the cut to 50/100 does not surface it; it moves it 85 → 103 → 109 as more candidates outrank it.
  • Q12 shtrd.f::chunk_1 enters the vector stage at a cut of 50 (vector score 0.782) and lands at merged rank 113 (cut50) / 115 (cut100). It is a candidate at those cuts but ranks far below a 10-slot answer.

So candidate generation is not the lever for these two: the pool already contains them and the cap discards them. Their scores (0.78–0.80) sit below dozens of higher-scoring candidates, which is the ranking/score-saturation problem §11 identified, not a missing-candidate problem. Q05's proper fix remains admission of eqgrd.f (scored 0.35, below the 0.5 ingest gate, per §10); Q12's is a ranking change over an already-ingested answer.

Negative control and method

  • Reproduction. The cut30 arm is the tool's own merge of the vector, text and example stages. Rerun live through the real _search_code (monkeypatched capture, verify_tool.py), it matches its order exactly for 6 of 12 questions and differs only inside score ties for the rest, with the top-10 set identical for 11 of 12. Its Q06 nDCG@10 is 0.4226, reproducing §9's recorded embedding-order Q06 value of 0.423, and shtrd.f and eqgrd.f remain outside the top 30 in every arm — the declared control.
  • Labels. From labels.json (§9 depth 30) plus the census answers (denominated label 1): eqgrd.f::chunk_0 for Q05, and the two shtrd.f chunks for Q12. No other new candidate entered any variant's top 10, so no further candidate needed labelling. IDCG is built from the full labelled pool at depth 10.
  • Variant definitions. cutN calls the vector stage with k=N (its ANN cut is max(2N,50)) and leaves the text and example stages at 30. lex adds up to 60 candidates per query whose chunk text or routine name contains a query token (stopwords removed), scored by matched-token fraction. desc re-ranks the cut30 pool by cosine(query, embed(CodeFile description + routine name + chunk text)) computed in the scratch harness with the same remote encoder, never written to the graph.
  • Ties. The tool's rounded 3-decimal scores tie heavily; order within a tie is set-iteration order and can shift between processes. nDCG@10 is stable where tied candidates share a label, which holds for the reproduction above, but a tie-broken figure is the more robust long-run statistic.

Recommendation

  1. Do not pursue candidate-generation widening for Q05/Q12. A vector cut of 50 or 100 leaves both answers in the top-30 miss and moves the mean by less than 0.01 (0.1898 → 0.1817 / 0.1984); the bounded lexical term is neutral-to-negative (0.1845) at 4× the current latency. The answers are already generated and ranked out, so the §11 ranking fix (reserved vector slots with a tie-break, node docs-score-ties) and Q05's file admission are the levers that can actually reach them.
  2. The one large gain is description-beside-text embedding, but it fails its control. It raises the mean from 0.1898 to 0.4240 over the 12 code questions — the largest single effect here — yet it drops the Q06 control from 0.4226 to 0.2305, costs ~14.6 s per query (against 5 ms for the embedding order), and re-ranks only the top-30 pool rather than widening generation. Treat it as a controlled experiment to be gated on recovering Q06, not as a default, and prefer it as a Jev-rerank input (§9) if it is taken further.
