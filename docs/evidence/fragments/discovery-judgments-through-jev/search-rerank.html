Search rerank — the shared pool core, and the pool size it runs at

The pool rerank lives in one core, rerank_pool in imas_codex/discovery/base/judgment.py, beside decide_batch. It takes the query, the retrieved pool, a builder that produces one judgement state per candidate, the five score levels, and the budget, concurrency, service and model; it judges the pool through decide_batch and returns the pool ordered by descending score, with a candidate that was unscored keeping its own retrieval position. The search tools keep rerank_candidates as the thin search-side caller that owns the judgement state (path, title, first 1500 characters of text) and the facility-discovery seat, so find_code and both search tools behave exactly as before. The core imports nothing from imas_codex.llm, asserted by a test that parses its import statements, which is what lets a discovery-side caller reuse it without depending on the search package.

The rerank's gain against the embedding order it replaces

The deployed tools rerank the retrieved pool through rerank_candidates. To show that the tool's own gain is reproduced, the sweep scores the same 18 JT-60SA questions twice on the same retrieval, labels and relevance rule: once in the tool's own embedding order (the order a search returns before any rerank) and once after the rerank. Why the deployed nDCG@10 is not the prototype's 0.509

The prototype's rerank mean nDCG@10 was 0.5085 (embedding 0.2457) over its own labelled candidate set: it labelled the top 30 its own retrieval returned and scored binary, with every candidate in that set carrying a label. The deployed figure is lower because the label set differs, not because the tool differs. The sweep's label rule adds the four census answers and counts every retrieved candidate outside the prototype's 30 as irrelevant, which grows IDCG and penalises the rerank for lifting candidates no label covers. Recomputed on the prototype's own labelled candidates alone, today's deployed rerank scores 0.4976 and today's embedding 0.2670 (emb 0.2670 -> 0.4976), reproducing the prototype's 0.2457 -> 0.5085 within 0.011; the residual is judge run-to-run variance and retrieval drift between the two days (431 of the prototype's 540 labelled ids are returned today). The whole-set figure is a different measurement on a different label set, and the two are not directly comparable.

Pool size: the deepest the 5 s budget can score

A deeper pool ranks the labelled candidates better and reaches more of them, so the pool is sized by the two metrics that do not carry the unlabelled-tail penalty: labelled-only nDCG@10 rises 0.4821 at 60 to 0.6529 at 150, and recall@10 rises 0.3772 to 0.4872. Both favour depth, and the only pool within 0.02 of the best labelled-only nDCG@10 (0.6529 at 150) is 150 itself. Latency, not the ranking, is what rules the deep pools out. Meeting the 5 s budget by raising the concurrency was tested live, and the decisions endpoint refuses to be sped up: at 48 judgements in flight the seat's per-call p50 rises from 1.0 s to 1.6 s and every pool's wall p95 passes 8 s, so the endpoint's throughput, not the in-flight bound, sets the floor. The four census answers need a pool the budget cannot afford

The census placed four answers the retrieval ranks out of reach: the poloidal flux grid (eqgrd.f), the plasma-current reader (shtrd.f), use_compiler-en and use_login-en. Reranking cannot recover what the retrieval never returned. At the chosen pool of 60 none of the four is retrieved, so all four are absent from the reranked report; they enter only at deeper pools, and there the rerank lifts them hard. use_login-en enters at pool 100 (reranked rank 2) and is rank 1 at pool 150; eqgrd.f and use_compiler-en enter at pool 150 (ranks 19 and 21); shtrd.f is absent at every pool — a retrieval miss, not a rerank miss.

Checks

The scoped gate (search tools, the decision call layer, the new core, the domain queries) runs green at the head that carries the pool change (gate.log). The negative control reuses the production test against a copy whose rerank is replaced by an identity, so the tool returns the embedding order unchanged: it exits non-zero and its first failure is the ordering assertion, with the lower-Score candidate before the higher-Score one (negative_control.log).
