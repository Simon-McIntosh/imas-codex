Search rerank — the shared pool core, and the pool size it runs at

The pool rerank now lives in one core, rerank_pool in imas_codex/discovery/base/judgment.py, beside decide_batch. It takes the query, the retrieved pool, a builder that produces one judgement state per candidate, the five score levels, and the budget, concurrency, service and model; it judges the pool through decide_batch and returns the pool ordered by descending score, with a candidate that was unscored keeping its own retrieval position. The search tools keep rerank_candidates as the thin search-side caller that owns the judgement state (path, title, first 1500 characters of text) and the facility-discovery seat, so find_code and both search tools behave exactly as before. The core imports nothing from imas_codex.llm, asserted by a test that parses its import statements, which is what lets a discovery-side caller reuse it without depending on the search package.

It has the best nDCG@10 of any size (0.3671 against 0.2775 at 60, 0.2872 at 100, 0.2343 at 150), and it is also the best of the two sizes whose p95 wall time fits the 5 s budget, so the smallest pool within 0.02 of the best under that constraint is 30 under either reading of the ranking: ranking the labelled candidates alone gives 0.4637 at 30 against 0.4493 at 60, still the best of the affordable pair. Cost falls with it, from $0.097 to $0.019 per 18 queries. The 100 that shipped before this measurement was set by the prototype's retrieval size and was never affordable at a 5 s budget: at 100 the p95 wall time is 6.27 s, so the budget truncates the slower half of the queries and leaves their candidates in retrieval order.

The four census answers need a pool the budget cannot afford

The census placed four answers the retrieval ranks out of reach: the poloidal flux grid — (eqgrd.f, deep rank 159), the plasma-current reader (shtrd.f, beyond 150 and absent from every pool this sweep retrieved), use_compiler-en (173) and use_login-en (181). Reranking cannot recover what the retrieval never returned. Where an answer did enter the pool the rerank lifted it hard: use_login-en sits at reranked rank 2 at pool 100 and rank 1 at pool 150. shtrd.f's absence is a retrieval miss, not a rerank miss, and no pool sizes fix it.

The negative control reuses the production test against a copy whose rerank is replaced by an identity, so the tool returns the embedding order unchanged: it exits non-zero and its first failure is the ordering assertion, with the lower-Score candidate before the higher-Score one (negative_control.log, last line EXIT=1).
