In 1D (radial) equations, these are defined by the total fluxes of particles, energy and momentum, across a flux surface given by : V' [-D Y' <|grad(rho_tor_norm)|^2> + v Y <|grad(rho_tor_norm)|>], where Y represents the particles, energy and momentum density, respectively, while V is the volume inside a flux surface, the primes denote derivatives with respect to rho_tor_norm and < X > is the flux surface average of a quantity X. This formulation remains valid when changing simultaneously rho_tor_norm into rho_tor in the gradient terms and in the derivatives denoted by the prime. The average flux stored in the IDS as sibling of D and v is the total flux described above divided by the flux surface area V' <|grad(rho_tor_norm)|>. Note that the energy flux includes the energy transported by the particle flux, in this form: Q = V' [- n D (T)' <|grad(rho_tor_norm)|^2> + v (nT) <|grad(rho_tor_norm)|>] + flux_multiplier * T * particle_flux. Fluxes and convection are positive (resp. negative) when outwards i.e. towards the LCFS (resp. inwards i.e. towards the magnetic axes).
