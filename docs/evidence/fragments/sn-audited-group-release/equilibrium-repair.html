Minimum safety factor is the least signed value of the safety factor over the specified equilibrium domain. It selects the global minimum of the winding ratio, not the value where its magnitude is smallest.

The defining relation is

$$
q_{\min} = \min_{\rho\in D} q(\rho)
$$

where $q_{\min}$ is the minimum safety factor, $q$ is the signed safety factor, $\rho$ labels magnetic flux surfaces, and $D$ is the supplied equilibrium domain expressed in that label.

In axisymmetry, or using straight-field-line flux coordinates $(\rho,\theta,\phi)$, the underlying winding ratio satisfies $q = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint \frac{\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\phi}{\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\theta}\,d\theta$, where $\mathbf{B}$ is the magnetic field, $\nabla$ is the spatial gradient operator, $\theta$ is the poloidal angle, and $\phi$ is the toroidal angle. The integral follows a magnetic field line through one complete poloidal circuit. The toroidal angle belongs to the right-handed cylindrical $(R,\phi,Z)$ frame, where $R$ is major radius and $Z$ is vertical height; $\phi$ increases counterclockwise when viewed from above.

The minimization covers the closed magnetic surfaces in $D$, including axis or boundary limits only where included and defined. It is neither an average nor a sum over surfaces. For a domain with safety-factor values $-4$ and $-1$, the minimum is $-4$, not $-1$.

Sign convention: Positive when magnetic field lines advance toward increasing toroidal angle as the poloidal angle increases on every surface in the minimization domain.
