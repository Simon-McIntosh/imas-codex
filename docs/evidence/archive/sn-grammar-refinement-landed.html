The migration premise, proven rather than assumed

The programme rests on one claim: an existing name can be moved to a new spelling without re-deriving its meaning. A no-write shadow comparison established it before anything was renamed, rendering every live identity under both the current and the proposed templates with the graph held fixed. 350 identities would change, all 350 proposals were free of collision with zero landing on a live or retired identity and zero many-to-one merges, and 331 were already publishable. The comparison cost no LLM call at all.

It also produced the evidence that narrowed the rule, which is the part worth keeping. Split by locus tail, 148 prefix and 6 postfix changes carry an of_, at_ or due_to_ tail and read better, while 145 prefix and 38 mixed changes carry no tail and read worse — accumulated_ammonia_count becoming ammonia_count_accumulated. Arity had been the proposed discriminator and was wrong; the tail is the discriminator, and tail-gating cut the surface from 350 to 165.

The reuse map, and the cause it located in another plan

A read-only scout mapped what already exists for both halves of the programme: 18 candidate mechanisms, 18 fitness verdicts, 0 unverdicted, and all 22 declared segment classes routed to their owning vocabulary file. Full record: reuse map.

Its decisive finding belongs to the WEST release rather than to this plan. The out-of-manifest leak that halted two candidates is run_review_release computing its names list as the union of mint_sn_list().names with the terminal manifest identities, while mint_sn_list deliberately adds each name's parent, siblings and children on top of its authoritative PRODUCED_NAME join. So the 150 unbound names were neither a near-miss at the manifest boundary nor a defect in mint_sn_list: that function is correct for the review-cohort contract it documents and wrong as a release selector. One union is replaced; the function is not touched.

Two findings shape this plan's own gates. Typed vocabulary registries get strict validation and cross-registry duplicate refusal at load time, but flat-list vocabularies have no equivalent load-time schema — so a syntactically valid, semantically misclassified token round-trips happily and is caught only by an assertion on the intended IR segment. And the migration path is lossless end to end with no LLM call: lenient parse() of the stored spelling, compose() of its IR, then apply_edit with rename at scope self, routing into persist_refined_name, which provisions the successor, creates lineage, supersedes the predecessor and migrates authoritative source bindings in one commit.
