The migration premise, proven rather than assumed

The programme rests on one claim: an existing name can be moved to a new spelling without re-deriving its meaning. A no-write shadow comparison established it before anything was renamed, rendering every live identity under both the current and the proposed templates with the graph held fixed. 350 identities would change, all 350 proposals were free of collision with zero landing on a live or retired identity and zero many-to-one merges, and 331 were already publishable. The comparison cost no LLM call at all.

It also produced the evidence that narrowed the rule, which is the part worth keeping. Split by locus tail, 148 prefix and 6 postfix changes carry an of_, at_ or due_to_ tail and read better, while 145 prefix and 38 mixed changes carry no tail and read worse — accumulated_ammonia_count becoming ammonia_count_accumulated. Arity had been the proposed discriminator and was wrong; the tail is the discriminator, and tail-gating cut the surface from 350 to 165.

The reuse map, and the cause it located in another plan

A read-only scout mapped what already exists for both halves of the programme: 18 candidate mechanisms, 18 fitness verdicts, 0 unverdicted, and all 22 declared segment classes routed to their owning vocabulary file. Full record: reuse map.

Its decisive finding belongs to the WEST release rather than to this plan. The out-of-manifest leak that halted two candidates is run_review_release computing its names list as the union of mint_sn_list().names with the terminal manifest identities, while mint_sn_list deliberately adds each name's parent, siblings and children on top of its authoritative PRODUCED_NAME join. So the 150 unbound names were neither a near-miss at the manifest boundary nor a defect in mint_sn_list: that function is correct for the review-cohort contract it documents and wrong as a release selector. One union is replaced; the function is not touched.

Two findings shape this plan's own gates. Typed vocabulary registries get strict validation and cross-registry duplicate refusal at load time, but flat-list vocabularies have no equivalent load-time schema — so a syntactically valid, semantically misclassified token round-trips happily and is caught only by an assertion on the intended IR segment. And the migration path is lossless end to end with no LLM call: lenient parse() of the stored spelling, compose() of its IR, then apply_edit with rename at scope self, routing into persist_refined_name, which provisions the successor, creates lineage, supersedes the predecessor and migrates authoritative source bindings in one commit.

The merged head, measured twice

Each change in this programme was green in its own worktree. None had been measured with the others. That gap is not hypothetical — it cost 27 added failures.

The 27 were attributed by evidence, and they did not fall where this plan would have guessed. Six came from the tail rule, one from registering the Stokes indices, nineteen from a consumer eligibility guard whose fixtures had never supplied a node_category, and one from a live-graph unit disagreement with no supported commit attribution at all. The nineteen were a coordinator gating error: that node was gated on tests/core and its focused surfaces, which its change passed cleanly, while the change itself was to source eligibility — reached by the standard-names extraction path. A gate has to be derived from what a change can reach, not from where its code lives.

The closure is on evidence, not arithmetic. The two repairs reported 19 and 7 removals from a 32-failure head, and 32 − 26 = 6 is exactly what the final run found. That subtraction was still not the evidence: a sum of two isolated deltas is a prediction, and only measuring the combined state establishes it. Both repairs kept their subject intact — the eligibility guard was never weakened, and every moved assertion still checks what it was written to check.

The sixth remaining failure is held open on purpose. Refreshing that holdout to the raw Data Dictionary value would fix in place a number that a resolution-authority repair might reverse, so it stays red with a stated reason rather than green with a hidden decision.

Neither rejection rested on the token feeling marginal. This programme carries a deliberate inclusion bias, because two valid bases were wrongly dismissed on it before. A rejection therefore needs positive evidence that an existing spelling carries the meaning — and in both cases that evidence was produced by spelling it: energy_flux_maximum_at_inner_divertor_target was shown to strict-parse and round-trip with a flux base and an energy channel, and the strain-gauge carriers were pinned by six round-trip tests plus one asserting the rejected spelling does not resolve.

The arithmetic is worth stating plainly. Two rejections cost one render-table example and a handful of pinning tests. Two duplicate bases would have been a permanent fork in a closed vocabulary, which every future name would have to be spelled around.

The headline figures re-measured: one current, seven drifted, one stale

Every headline number in the parent plan was re-read from the live graph and the installed grammar at base 5ea0d73b, read-only, before the sprint is rescoped on them. The direction is uniform and expected: the plan's figures were written before its own migration, so where the cohort or the operator surface grew the figure drifted up, and where this plan's own work landed the figure is not drifted but stale.

The current verdict is a replication, which is why it is the strongest row. The closing census here classified its 2,360-name cohort as 2,352 stable and 8 refused. An independent instrument — parse each live name, compose its intermediate representation back to a string, keep the names whose spelling does not survive the round trip — finds exactly 8 accepted live names rendering away from their stored spelling, and they are the same eight identities the census refused. The graph now carries 2,163 additional retired identities and 607 additional live ones, and the count has not moved.
