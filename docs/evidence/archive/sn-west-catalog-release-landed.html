WEST catalog release — cumulative evidence

This record consolidates what the sn-west-catalog-release plan proved while building a review surface a physicist can actually read. It is an evidence ledger, not a release claim: the fork candidate is a review artifact, and the human review plus the upstream leg remain ahead. Every result below is reconciled against the committed run ledger at docs/state/imas-codex/crew.json and the reports the runs left on disk under ~/.config/reckon/crew/reports/.

Every review-surface defect was fixed at the authority, never worked around

Four defects made the first candidates unreviewable, and each one was traced to the imas-standard-names package rather than patched in the codex exporter, on the lead's explicit direction. A physics domain could not be carried by a catalog entry at all, so the review site grouped every name under Uncategorized; the fix makes physics_domain a first-class required field of the entry model. The exporter was writing its own YAML, so review formatting could drift from what the schema accepted; the fix moves emission into the package's own dump_catalog_yaml. Prose then arrived as one unreadable line per paragraph; the fix emits description and documentation as folded blocks whose paragraphs fold near 80 columns while equations, display-math fences, list items and blank lines keep their authored layout, with a byte-identical reload proven over a 300-character paragraph carrying display math. Three physical bases were registered with the wrong structural kind, which is what put tensor beside a flux-surface-averaged gradient magnitude.

Stored kinds reconciled to zero disagreements, and an earlier sweep repaired

Structural kind is derived from the canonical identity, but the value stored on each name is a snapshot taken when the name was composed. A snapshot taken against a registry that mis-classified three bases is wrong in both directions, so the reconcile had to fire after the pin moved. The lead locked that ordering, and it mattered: a reconcile had already run at 16:49 UTC under the 0.8.2 pin and flipped 40 correct vectors to scalar among its 181 rows. The single unscoped drain under the 0.8.3 pin repaired them.

Negative result: four verified workers were lost to an exhausted metered quota

Late in the session every OpenAI-backed lane reported 100% weekly utilisation with a reset six days out, and the backend dropped four in-flight sessions mid-turn. One had already accepted six of ten staged names for 0.42 USD before it died. None of the four committed. The recovery was not to wait: each run was ledgered as an infrastructure failure with its partial outcome recorded, the trivial mechanical nodes were repointed at the locally served backend, and the judgement-heavy nodes at a frontier Claude backend registered for exactly this case. A distinct backend entry is required rather than an effort override, because a roster session must never cross models.

Two defects the cut exposed, both closed the same night

The release created the pull request and the tag, then exited 1 at its own preview-link invariant. The invariant was right and the transport under it was wrong: the body update shelled out to gh pr edit, which now fails against this repository with the Projects-classic GraphQL deprecation and leaves the body unchanged. Every batch release would have opened a pull request with no preview address for the reviewer to follow. The body update now issues a REST patch, proven by three new tests with 104 passing in the focused release suites.

The second defect was quieter. Twenty live names carried no physics domain at all, and since the field became required the exporter substituted a placeholder that catalog validation rejects — which is how one name left the batch as an invalid entry rather than as a physics judgement. The recovery took each name's domain from its bound data-dictionary source where one existed and from the configured classifier seat otherwise, never hand-picked. Null domains went from 20 to 0, every written value an enum member, each with exactly one matching domain edge and a recorded change; 4694 of 4694 names now carry a domain. Sixteen of the twenty were superseded or exhausted, which says no domain route had ever run over non-live stages.

The recut's blocker was a physics error in the plan, not a missing rename

The plan asked for four names to be steered onto a base carrying per-volume per-solid-angle structure. Checked against the data dictionary, the four are three different shapes, and only one of them is that shape at all. The corpus census of accepted bases by unit is what settles each case: m^-3.s^-1 has source_rate on 13 accepted names against emissivity 2 and density 1; m^-3.s^-1.sr^-1 has zero accepted names; and m^-2.s^-1.sr^-1 has photon_radiance 3, brightness 2 and factor 1.

So source_rate was the wrong direction for two of the four: their unit is a radiance per unit wavelength, and asserting a volumetric production rate would claim something the path does not measure. Because their end state is known and parses, they are --rename redesigns rather than --hint steers — a cheaper and more determinate route than the one the plan specified.

The fourth name has no legal spelling, and the refusals are exact. hard_xray_source_rate_per_solid_angle, hard_xray_source_rate_per_steradian, hard_xray_angular_source_rate and hard_xray_photon_emissivity each raise UnknownBaseTokenError against the 211-token closed vocabulary at the pinned 0.8.8 grammar, and volumetric_hard_xray_source_rate raises NonCanonicalNameError. Nothing in 176 physical-base or 114 qualifier tokens means per unit solid angle: the vocabulary carries spectral, bremsstrahlung, volumetric, viewing and direction, and no per_solid_angle, steradian or angular. The only legal spellings drop the steradian, leaving the unit string as the sole place the physics survives. On the lead's ruling the vocabulary gains the qualifier instead — the mass_flow_rate precedent — which fixes every future per-solid-angle quantity at the same time and withholds this one name from the current candidate.

Two operational facts came out of the same measurement. A 120-second tool ceiling, not the 45-minute budget, killed the one real inline review, so any node running a governed review must allow well beyond that. And the killed review stranded one claim token, which was released through the governed route with a graph-wide count confirming zero others — the ceiling has not been quietly accumulating claims.

One transport for GitHub, one route for a physics domain, one honest cascade surface

Three release-path residues the review surface had left behind are closed, each gated on a measure rather than a reading.

Every pull-request call now speaks REST through a single seam. Nine gh-CLI call sites went over two commits: creation, body read-back, closed-head lookup and body update in the release module (75a5d2ca, grep '"gh",' 4 to 0, 113 focused tests green with 11 added), then resolve_merged_pr, fetch_pr_evidence, the CI check read, the changelog contributor lookup and the graph-quality dispatch (9da911db, 2/2/0 to 0/0/0, 110 tests green with 11 added). The transport was consolidated into imas_codex/graph/ghcr.py, which is now the only urllib request builder for GitHub anywhere in the repository. The motivating exposure was concrete: the GitHub CLI resolves pull-request metadata through GraphQL, which fails outright on a repository still carrying Projects-classic fields — the defect that had already forced the body-update path off the CLI. A third call site the followup never named, _closed_pr_heads, was swept in with the rest. gh is no longer needed for any of these reads, though a credential must still resolve; gh auth token remains the last fallback, so an operator authenticated through the CLI is unaffected.

One route writes a physics domain. reclassify_domain in edit.py had no caller anywhere and wrote the property without the HAS_PHYSICS_DOMAIN edge or a transactional guard — a footgun for whoever found it before the atomic version. Deleted at d36d8b6c, 78 lines, leaving the ledger operation string and the generated docstrings untouched.

The cascade surface stopped reporting deferred work as done (9e4773f7, 99 passed with 12 added). A subtree rename plans its descendants with dry_run=True and persists none of them until the root reaches accepted, while the report printed them under a bare "planned renames" heading — so an exhausted root left the operator believing the work had happened. Fixed at the report, never at the gate: no acceptance gate was loosened and no write path was added.

The second half of that node found the unreachable in cascade — no parent in plan message had been blaming topology for a deliberate decision. On a strictly linear chain the middle node leaves the plan on purpose — a locus edge is a semantic boundary — and the builder then classified its child as an unresolvable fault, which refused the whole edit because conflicts are all-or-nothing. Reproduced on temperature ← temperature_of_plasma ← ion_temperature_of_plasma. The walk now records why each descendant left, at 11 exit sites, and carries that closure downward. One consequence to know: a subtree below a boundary or a safety refusal now lands in skipped rather than conflicts, so an edit previously refused end-to-end will proceed and rename the reachable part.

The suite was measured at the merged head carrying the first two of these: 6943 collected, 6932 passed, 8 skipped, 3 failed, added failures 0, with the three failing ids exactly the pre-existing set on the export and publish gating surface.

One merge was deliberately held. The REST-consolidation node reported collateral outside its own write fence, and it reproduced exactly: two test files patch promote.subprocess.run, which those paths no longer call, so the mocks are inert and the tests reach the real network — failing on an HTTP 404. Merging the wiring alone would have added two failures to main, the mistake this plan has already recorded once. The reconciliation was dispatched on top of the commit so the two land together, green where either alone leaves two.

The provenance leak: found, measured, plugged, and made visible

The lead asked for the source-stripping to be fixed at root. The first answer was incomplete and the second came from the lead reading physics rather than reasoning. An orphan triage reported two sourceless names as sound detachments needing their pairings retired; the lead challenged both and the data dictionary settled both against the report. summary/global_quantities/power_steady/value is documented as "the total coupled power minus the time derivative of the stored energy", which is exactly the name that was refused it; and equilibrium/time_slice/constraints/n_e_line is keyworded interferometry, which is exactly the locus the guard said it did not share.

Both rules were judging a path's spelling rather than what the dictionary says the path measures. The tense rule read a temporal operator anywhere in a name as making the whole quantity a rate, ignoring that it sat inside one operand of a binary difference. The locus rule looked for a literal device token in the path string.

The gap that made the leak invisible was separate and worse. Whether the tail was closing or regenerating was unanswerable until 508 records were dated by hand. Attribution now runs end to end: sink, both call sites, and the tests that assert them, merged as one change because either half alone leaves the suite with two failures.

What remains, and what blocks it

The physicist review is the human gate the whole sprint is aimed at, and nothing downstream of it can start early. After the review returns: the reviewed disposition folds back through sn approve, the upstream catalog pull request and its stable tag follow, and the undo rehearsal proves the fold is reversible. The withheld names follow as a later additive batch under their recorded reason groups.

The release chain is closed: standard-names reached v0.8.8 across eight releases, each closing a defect the review surface exposed, with both consumers pinned and the environment verified. The release recipe in imas_codex/standard_names/AGENTS.md gates pin-before-reconcile with a runnable census and gates the candidate on the published bytes, so a session that has not lived through this one can cut the next candidate without rediscovering any of it.

The recut carries three of the four blocked names. Two are radiance quantities the plan had mis-typed and one needs only the established source_rate base; all three parse under the pinned grammar. The fourth, hard_xray_rate, waits on a solid-angle qualifier entering the ISN closed vocabulary, which runs fork pull request, upstream pull request, a tag the lead publishes to PyPI, then a pin bump at both sites. It is withheld from this candidate and enters the next.

Behind that: the 1190 unclaimed extracted sources are slow rather than expensive — under a dollar of model spend but hours of wall clock, so they want a long background drain rather than budget.
