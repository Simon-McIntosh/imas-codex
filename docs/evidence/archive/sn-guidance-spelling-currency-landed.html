The pointers were replaced, not re-pinned, and that is the whole result. Three of the five had rotted within hours of a single rename that shifted the schema by roughly twenty lines: 3014-3021 cited for produced_sn_id resolved to provenance, 3346-3349 for terminal_sn_id to before_closure_hash, and 2995-2999 to unrelated vocab_gap prose. Restoring the numbers would have fixed the symptom and scheduled the identical failure at the next slot insertion. A class-and-slot reference survives any edit that does not rename the slot, and a reader can grep it.

What did not move matters as much as what did. The migration rule covers declared generic foreign keys only, so produced_sn_id and terminal_sn_id are correct and were fenced out — their counts were measured before and after rather than assumed. The 318 bare sn_id tokens in graph_ops.py and 190 in workers.py are Python parameters, locals, dataclass fields and UNWIND row keys: a different namespace, untouched. A sweep that "unified the foreign key" without that fence would have produced an enormous diff, changed no graph behaviour, and put the hottest writers at risk.

A pointer is not a spelling, and the distinction was worth getting right

This work was first raised as a shipped plan having landed with a false done-when clause. That charge did not hold and was withdrawn. The clause covers spellings under AGENTS.md, agents/ and imas_codex/**/AGENTS.md, and those files are free of sn_id, sn_ids, docs_review_resolution_method and the plural axis token; where they name produced_sn_id and terminal_sn_id they are correct, because both are still declared.

What rotted was a pointer, and a spelling grep cannot see a line number by construction. The real defect is narrower and more useful: the measure was narrower than the standing rule it served, which already requires that a cited anchor be opened and confirmed to exist. The schema's own prose was never in the scanned set at all — it is neither an AGENTS.md nor under agents/, yet it is the authority every other file cites.
