The local-lane concurrency knee on review-sized payloads is 32, not 4

What was measured. The served endpoint (http://98dci4-gpu-0003:18802/v1, sglang, max_model_len 512000, model deepseek-v4.1-flash) was driven with review-sized payloads: the real sn/review_names_system and sn/review_names_user templates rendered through render_prompt over build_compose_context(), giving 17,173 prompt tokens and 694 completion tokens per request, at reasoning_effort="medium" and max_tokens=700. Forty distinct user payloads were built from forty real StandardName identities read from the live graph, so the requests are not identical and the shared system prefix is the only cached span — which is the production shape of a review pass.

The result. Throughput rises through 32 concurrent requests and then flattens: 9.11 req/min at 1, 21.32 at 4, 26.76 at 8, 29.74 at 16, 36.45 at 32, and 34.30 at 64, the c=64 point taken from a run whose 64 requests were all emitted and completed. Aggregate completion throughput saturates near 400 tokens per second (105 → 247 → 312 → 347 → 425 → 400). The knee, as the lowest level beyond which throughput does not rise, is 32: the doubling to 64 falls 5.9% against the same 36.45 the curve plots at 32, so the curve flattens rather than rises.

Why it flattens where it does, established after the fact. Every concurrency in this record is requested — what the client held outstanding — and the engine does not run that many. Its launch carries max_running_requests=36, confirmed for this engine and for its predecessor from both launch lines, with schedule_policy='fcfs', max_total_tokens=4000000 and hicache_ratio=2.0. So the requested c=64 row ran 36 in the engine with about 28 queued inside it, not 64 concurrently, and the knee at 32 is the last level that fits under that cap. The flattening is therefore the cap asserting itself rather than the GPUs saturating, which is a stronger statement than the curve alone supports: asking for more than 36 does not buy throughput because the engine will not run more than 36.

That also re-reads the starvation figure. The 43 of 64 requests past the 60 s deadline at c=64 are largely the internally queued population waiting behind a full running set under first-come ordering, not requests thrashing against the GPUs. A queue ordered by cached prefix rather than arrival is exactly what would move that number, so this row is the one to compare against any future scheduling change.

The spread is measured, not assumed, and it is why the criterion is the plateau rather than a single point. Four samples in the record carry an achieved concurrency of 32: the 36.45 req/min at the c=32 level, the two identical-load repeats at 32.01 and 32.98, and a first attempt at c=64 that emitted only 32 requests, whose 26.13 req/min therefore belongs to c=32 on its achieved concurrency. Across all four the band is 10.32 req/min wide — 32% of their mean — and the c=64 point at 34.30 sits inside it, which is the sense in which the plateau is flat rather than a fall.

That mis-dispatched attempt is held out of the curve, not out of the spread, and the two exclusions are separate. Its own concurrency field reads 64 while its n_requests is 32, so it cannot supply the c=64 point: the c=64 value the curve plots comes from a run that emitted and completed all 64 requests. It is recorded under rejected_samples for that reason alone. Naming a knee from that single pass would have read 26.13 req/min at c=64 — 28% below the 36.45 the curve plots at 32 — reporting a collapse above 32 that does not exist.

Timeout rate. Every level records timeout_rate_900s 0.0 and zero errors, so none of the requests the record carries hit the pipeline's own local-endpoint deadline of 900 s (MODEL_TOKEN_LIMITS in imas_codex/discovery/base/llm.py). The record carries 264 requests in all: 168 across the six measured levels, 64 in the two c=32 repeats, and 32 in the mis-dispatched attempt held out of the curve. That deadline is too loose to be the instrument near this knee, so the starvation column records requests exceeding 60 s — eight times the solo latency, the shape a stalled slot takes. What this resolves and what it does not. The two prior readings were both honest and neither governs this workload. The compose-path probe found its knee at 4 because a compose batch generates ~18.5k tokens, so it is decode-bound end to end; a review request generates ~694 tokens and is instead prompt-dominated, and the served variant adds prefix caching over the shared system span. On review-sized payloads the knee is 32, which confirms the lead's ~30 for this instrument rather than the compose default of 4 — and it is the number the instrument's default should be set from. The caution that survives is the shape above 4: each doubling past 4 buys 26%, 11% and 23% while per-request latency grows roughly in proportion, so concurrency buys aggregate throughput at the cost of every individual request's wall time.

Instrument limits, stated. The load generator is network-I/O bound and ran on the login node; it issues HTTP against the served endpoint and performs no local CPU work. Neither nvidia-smi on the serving node nor its /metrics gauge was reachable from here (SSH key exchange is refused, /metrics answers empty through the router), so no server-side concurrency gauge corroborates the client-side in-flight count; achieved concurrency is asserted from the emitted request counts plus the flat plateau, and the c=32 repeats are the control on that. A concurrent worker on this lane would appear as spread of exactly the kind measured, so the 32% band is the upper bound the conclusion is held to.
