Mapping candidate retrieval and Jev pair judgment

The test-split measurement that §4 and §5 of mapping-candidate-selection are sized from. It answers two questions on the 75-source adjudicated test split (36 labelled sources over 42 current DD 4.1.1 nodes, 39 none with a reason): whether an IDS-routing step first improves the DD shortlist, and whether Jev's pair judgments can select a source's target by themselves or only pre-screen it. The labels are those of candidate-label-adjudication; the two labelling passes agreed exactly on 0.633 of the 30-source subsample. Every number below is on the test split. Tuning of the routing thresholds reads the dev split only.

§4 — Batching, final pick and cost

Measure 4. One call per source carrying K candidates and 2K questions, against one call per pair, on a 10-source test subsample. Mean absolute difference in same_quantity probability is 0.032. Batching is 4× cheaper ($0.000215 vs $0.000862 per source) and 12× faster (0.53 s vs 6.52 s mean latency), so §5 builds the batched form.

Measure 5 (optional). A single Jev choice over the top 5 candidates plus none_of_these: 71 of 75 sources answered, and of those only 7 named a concrete path — the other 64 chose none_of_these. Of the 7 concrete picks 2 were correct (accuracy 0.286); 71 % agreed with the plain same_quantity argmax. The final pick adds nothing over argmax and is not adopted.

Measure 6. Jev pair judgment costs $0.000205 per source on the test split ($0.0145 for all 71 judged sources), the IDS-routing Choice $0.0195 for the 75, and none of this reads a reasoning-model seat. Projected to all 2,288 enriched JET sources, pair judgment is $0.47 and IDS routing about $0.60 — far inside the $3 measurement cap.

§5 — What the numbers support, and what §4 builds

Role: Jev as a pre-screen only. Jev ranks candidate paths well on this split — same_quantity AUC 0.924 and top-1 accuracy 0.80 among recoverable sources — and, combined with IDS routing, lifts shortlist recall@20 from 0.556 to 0.694. But its same_quantity probabilities are compressed: the highest score any test source reaches is 0.71 and the mean best score is 0.22, so no select threshold both clears the 0.9 precision bar and selects a useful share. The best precision with meaningful coverage is 0.625 at threshold 0.40, and precision reaches 0.9 only at a single selected source (1.3 %). The dev-tuned point confirms it: dev precision 1.0 over two sources falls to 0.40 over five on test. That is the signature of a good ranker with an uncalibrated absolute scale, so the candidate stage should flag and escalate, and the reasoning model should keep the final choice. The §5 decision resolves to pre-screen only: every non-floor source escalates with its shortlist.

What §4 builds. Retrieval keeps the maintained hybrid_dd_search and adds the Jev IDS-routing Choice in front of it — top-3 IDSs as ids_filter, hits ranked by blended score. K = 20: recall has largely saturated by 20 (0.694 at 20 against 0.722 at 50) and the shortlist is what §5 asks Jev to judge, so a longer list buys candidates Jev cannot separate. The unit-dimension filter and the cluster modifier are left out of the retrieval stage for now, since neither moves recall at K ≤ 20 on this sample and the unit filter is untestable at four labelled sources with a representative unit.

§6 — Frozen question text

The wording is fixed as the plan's same_quantity and same_object:

  • same_quantity (noul): "Would the IMAS field at candidate.path correctly hold the values that signal_source provides?" True: storing them there needs at most a unit conversion, a sign or COCOS flip, or the selection of one array element. False: the field holds a different quantity, a different component or coordinate, a different object, or only a related or derived quantity.
  • same_object (noul): "Does the structure containing candidate.path describe the same kind of object as the source?" True: the same kind of object. False: a different kind of object, even if the quantity matches (a PF coil current against a passive-loop current is an example).

In the batched form the questions reference candidates[i].path against the candidate array carried in state; the pair form uses candidate.path.
